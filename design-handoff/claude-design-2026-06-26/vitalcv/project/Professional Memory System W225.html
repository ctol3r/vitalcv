D1

Career Event Architecture

the atom of a professional life

A career is not a document — it is a sequence of events, and the CareerEvent is the atom that records each one. Where the EvidenceObject (W215) captures a fact that is true, the CareerEvent captures a moment that happened: a graduation, a license issued, a promotion, an award. Every event is dated, sourced, and typed; it enters the record once and never leaves it. The architecture has three parts — a taxonomy that classes every kind of professional moment, a lifecycle every event passes through from anticipated to historical, and the impact each event carries: what trust it confers and what mobility it unlocks. Get the atom right and the whole memory above it composes cleanly.

Event lifecycle · the five states every CareerEvent passes through
Anticipated → Occurred → Verified → Recorded → Historicalappend-only · never deleted
STATE 01
Anticipated

Expected, not yet real. The pathway predicts it — a board exam scheduled, a fellowship to finish. Visible as a future node; confers nothing yet.

STATE 02
Occurred

It happened. The date is real but the proof is not yet in hand — self-reported or pending source. It exists in memory, provisionally.

STATE 03
Verified

Proven at primary source. The event is corroborated and now confers its full trust and mobility impact. This is the state that counts.

STATE 04
Recorded

Committed to memory. Written to the permanent ledger with its source and timestamp. Immutable — corrections append, they never overwrite.

STATE 05
Historical

True, but past. A role ended, a license lapsed. The event never leaves the record — it moves from the active present into durable history.

The lifecycle is append-only · an event advances forward through states and is never deleted — even a superseded or corrected event stays in the ledger, marked, so the history of the record is itself remembered.
Event impact · what each event confers
Trust impactwhich dimensions it lifts

Every verified event raises or refreshes specific trust dimensions. A Board Certification lifts Authority and sets a milestone floor; an Employment Start with a clean reference lifts Institutional; a Publication lifts Research. Impact is typed to the event class — the taxonomy decides which dimension moves, and verification decides whether it moves at all.

Mobility impactwhich gates it opens

Every verified event clears gates in the Mobility Engine (W230). Board Certification opens the privileges gate; leadership tenure opens the director gate; a terminal degree opens the faculty branch. An event is not just remembered — it is spent: it changes which opportunities a career can now cross into, the instant it is recorded.

Deliverable · CareerEvent ArchitectureA CareerEvent is a dated, sourced, typed record of one professional moment — classed by a six-part taxonomy, advanced through a five-state append-only lifecycle, and carrying a typed trust and mobility impact. It is the atom of professional memory: small, immutable, and composable. Everything above it — the memory model, the timeline, recognition, trust over time — is just CareerEvents, accumulated and read.
D2

Professional Memory Model

five layers of a remembered career

If CareerEvents are the atoms, ProfessionalMemory is the structure they accumulate into — and not all memory behaves the same way. ProfessionalMemory organizes a career into five layers, each holding a different kind of fact and obeying a different rule of time. The crucial insight is that memory and currency are separate: the event that a license was issued is permanent, but the status of that license decaying. The model keeps both — a durable historical fact and a living, decaying state — so a career can be both fully remembered and accurately current at once. Each layer below names what it holds and how time treats it: what persists forever, what decays, and what becomes historical.

The three questions of memory · what persists, decays, stays historical
ANSWER 01

Persists forever

Identity and the fact of every event. Who a clinician is, and that each event happened, never change. Graduation, a license once issued, an award once won — these are written once and are true forever. The permanent layer is the spine of the record: everything else hangs off facts that cannot be revised, only added to.

ANSWER 02

Decays

Currency, not events. What decays is the present-tense status of a fact — whether a license is active today, whether a reference is still fresh, whether a read is recent. Decay never erases the event; it lowers the event's contribution to current trust until it is refreshed. Time pulls a fact out of the present, not out of the record.

ANSWER 03

Stays historical

The past, preserved. A role that ended, a license that lapsed, a former title — these do not vanish and do not decay; they move into history as completed, true facts. Historical memory is what lets a 40-year career show its whole arc: every former position still legible, dated, and verifiable, long after it stopped being current.

Deliverable · Professional Memory ModelProfessionalMemory holds a career in five layers — Identity, Authority, Experience, Recognition, Reputation — and separates the permanent event from its decaying current status. Identity and recognition persist forever; authority and reputation keep a living status that decays without refresh; experience accretes and ages into history. The model remembers everything that happened while keeping an honest read of what is true now.
D3

Career Timeline Experience

one record, four lenses

Memory is structured to be stored; the timeline is structured to be seen. A career visualizes as a spine — events laid along the line of time, the shape of a working life at a glance. But four audiences need four different readings of the same spine, so the timeline is one record rendered through four lenses. A clinician sees their story and what is next; a recruiter sees trajectory and momentum; an employer sees tenure and stability; credentialing sees verifiable events and gaps. Nothing is hidden or invented per lens — the same CareerEvents are filtered, weighted, and annotated for what each reader came to learn.

Four lenses on one spine · what each reader sees
Lens · Clinicianmy story

The whole arc, plus what's next. The clinician sees growth, milestones reached, and the anticipated events ahead on their pathway. The timeline is a mirror and a map — where they have been, and the next rung within reach.

Lens · Recruitertrajectory

Momentum and direction. The recruiter sees the slope of the spine — how fast the career is climbing, where it is heading, recognition density. Tenure detail is dimmed; trajectory and fit signals are foregrounded.

Lens · Employerstability

Tenure and continuity. The employer sees how long roles were held and whether the line is unbroken — gaps, overlaps, departures. The story is read for steadiness, not ascent: will this person stay and perform.

Lens · Credentialingverifiability

Events and currency only. Credentialing sees each event's verification state and freshness, every gap flagged, every source resolvable. Narrative falls away; what remains is a checklist of provable, current facts.

As a spine of events across time. The line is time; the nodes are CareerEvents; the bands are eras. A career becomes a single legible shape — its formation, its climb, its recognitions — readable in one glance and drillable to any node.

As the slope and reach of the spine. Growth is the rise from formation to leadership to standing — denser recognition, higher gates cleared, longer tenure. The recruiter reads slope; the clinician reads the next rung; both see the same ascent.

D4

Recognition System

esteem conferred, not claimed

Most of a career is what a clinician did; recognition is what others decided about it — and that makes it a fundamentally different kind of memory. The Professional Recognition Event (PRE) records a moment when a third party conferred distinction: an award, a named appointment, election to a society, an honor. A PRE cannot be self-asserted — its entire value is that someone else, with standing, chose to grant it. This section expands the PRE into a structured object and draws the sharp lines that keep it honest: how recognition differs from evidence, from trust, and from reputation. Conflate them and the record inflates; separate them and recognition becomes a precise, scarce, durable signal.

Keeping the four separate is what stops a record from inflating its own importance.
D5

Trust Through Time

how forty years accumulate

Trust is never a single number — it is a curve drawn across a whole career. Earlier waves computed trust at a point in time; this deliverable models how that point moves over forty years. Trust reinforces as corroborating events accumulate, decays when evidence goes unrefreshed, recovers after a lapse or gap, and locks in milestones that permanently raise the floor it can never fall below. The full history — not just the current value — is the asset: it is what lets the system say not only how much a career can be trusted today, but how durably it has been trusted across decades. A long career is not a high score; it is a high, well-defended floor.

The four laws of trust through time
01
The floor only ratchets up.

A milestone permanently raises the minimum trust a career carries. floor(t) = max(milestones ≤ t) — it never decreases. Decay and gaps move current trust around above the floor, but a board-certified clinician never falls below a board-certified baseline.

02
Decay touches currency, not history.

What fades is the present-tense status of evidence, not the event or the floor beneath it. A lapsed license lowers today's trust but never erases that the license was held — the historical fact and the milestone it set both stand.

03
History outweighs the spike.

A long, steady curve is worth more than a recent jump to the same value. The system reads trust = f(current, history) — depth and consistency over decades are themselves a signal that a momentary high cannot fake.

04
Recovery starts at the floor, not at zero.

After any lapse, a career rebuilds from its milestone floor, not from nothing. The deeper the history, the faster the recovery — which is the mathematical meaning of "the benefit of the doubt a long career has earned."

A forty-year career accumulates trust as a high, well-defended floor with a living current line above it. Decades of standing become a baseline no single bad day can undo.

the success criteria

The five deliverables compose into one memory system. CareerEvents are the atoms; ProfessionalMemory is the structure they accumulate into; the timeline is how that structure is seen; recognition is the scarcest layer of it; and trust through time is how the whole thing earns belief across decades. Read together they answer the wave's question: how does a career become memory?

Q1What is Professional Memory?
A career held as structured, durable memory across time — five layers of accumulated CareerEvents. Identity, Authority, Experience, Recognition, and Reputation each hold a different kind of fact and age by a different rule. Memory remembers everything that happened while keeping an honest, decaying read of what is true now — the permanent event and its current status, both preserved.
Q2What is a Career Event?
The atom of a professional life — a dated, sourced, typed record of one moment that happened. It is classed by a six-part taxonomy, advanced through a five-state append-only lifecycle from anticipated to historical, and carries a typed impact: which trust dimensions it lifts and which mobility gates it opens. Everything above it is just CareerEvents, accumulated and read.
Q3What is Recognition?
Esteem a third party conferred — scarce, sourced, and impossible to self-claim. The Professional Recognition Event records who granted an honor, what it rewards, how selective it is, and how it ages. Recognition is not evidence (a fact you hold), not trust (belief in that fact), and not reputation (the aggregate): it is the one signal a clinician cannot manufacture, which is what gives it weight.
Q4How does trust evolve?
As a curve, not a number. Trust reinforces on each corroboration, decays when evidence goes unrefreshed, recovers from the floor after a lapse, and ratchets that floor permanently upward at milestones. floor(t)=max(milestones≤t) never falls; current trust breathes above it. The whole history — depth and consistency — is the asset, not the latest value.
Q5What follows a clinician forever?
Identity, every event that happened, every recognition conferred, and the milestone floor those events set. A role can end and a license can lapse — those move into history but never leave the record. What is permanent is the spine: who they are, what they did, what others granted them, and the trust baseline decades of milestones have earned. The present decays; the record does not.
Q6How does a career become reusable memory?
By being recorded as immutable, typed CareerEvents the moment they verify — so they never need re-proving. Each event is stored once, ages by its layer's correct rule, and is read on demand through whatever lens a recruiter, employer, or credentialer needs. A career proven once becomes memory that every future opportunity can draw on without asking the clinician to start over. VitalCV stops re-verifying the moment and starts remembering the life.
