D1

Reputation Doctrine

accumulated professional credibility

Professional Reputation is the durable, earned standing a clinician carries — the accumulated credibility that years of verified evidence and conferred recognition compound into. It is the aggregate, the fourth and final rung of a ladder where each step is the input to the next: a fact is observed (evidence), the fact is believed (trust), an honor is conferred (recognition), and over time these settle into standing (reputation). Reputation is not a separate primitive and is never stored as a number — it is computed from everything beneath it. The doctrine's whole job is to fix what may climb that ladder and what may not, because the difference between credibility and a popularity score is entirely a question of what you let in.

The credibility ladder · four rungs, each the input to the next
evidence → trust → recognition → reputation · the aggregate at the topeach rung feeds the one above
01OBSERVED
Evidence

A fact you hold. A source-bound assertion — a license, a degree, a publication. The raw material; true, but not yet weighed.

02COMPUTED
Trust

Belief in the fact. How corroborated and current the evidence is. Trust is what evidence earns once it is verified and fresh.

03CONFERRED
Recognition

Esteem others granted. A scarce honor a third party bestowed, that cannot be self-claimed. Trusted evidence, amplified by who noticed it.

04ACCUMULATED
Reputation

Standing over time. The aggregate of trusted evidence and conferred recognition, compounded across a career into durable credibility.

Reputation is the integral of the ladder over time · it is not a fifth kind of thing — it is what the first three become when they accumulate, corroborate, and persist for years.

The four doctrinal questions · what may move reputation, and what may never
Creates

What creates reputation

Verified evidence and conferred recognition, accumulated over time. Reputation is built by doing the work and having it proven — a body of verified events, milestones reached, honors that others bestowed, and trust that has stayed high across years. It is earned slowly and only from facts.

verified eventsconferred recognitionsustained trustbreadth · depth
Destroys

What destroys reputation

Verified adverse facts — and only those. A substantiated investigation, a license restriction, a retraction, a falsification proven at source. Destruction runs on the same evidentiary bar as creation: a fall must be as provable as a rise. Allegation alone moves nothing until it is verified.

license restrictionsubstantiated findingretraction · fraud
Reinforces

What reinforces reputation

Consistency, corroboration, and recency. The same standing held steadily for years outweighs a recent spike; independent sources agreeing strengthen the read; continued activity keeps it current. Reinforcement is the compounding interest of a career that keeps showing up and proving it.

years of consistencyindependent corroborationrecent activity
Never

What may never affect reputation

Anything that is not a verified professional fact. Popularity, followers, endorsements, opinions, self-promotion, connection count, volume of profile views — and every demographic attribute. None of it is credibility, so the engine refuses all of it by construction. This list is what stops the engine from becoming a social network.

popularityendorsementsopinionsfollowersdemographics

It is created by verified facts, destroyed only by verified adverse facts on the same evidentiary bar, reinforced by consistency and corroboration over time, and — by hard rule — moved by nothing that is merely popular, social, or opined. That refusal is the doctrine.
D2

Reputation Dimensions

credibility is domain-specific

Should reputation be a single number or many? The answer is decisive: many — because a single reputation score is a popularity ranking in disguise. A brilliant researcher may be an untested administrator; a beloved clinician may have never published. Collapsing those into one figure destroys the only information an evaluator actually needs — credibility at what. So reputation is a profile of seven independent dimensions, each computed from its own evidence classes, each standing on its own. There is no headline number stored anywhere; if a single figure is ever shown, it is composed at read time against a specific question. The profile, not the score, is the unit of reputation.

D3

Reputation Events

which moments move standing

Not every CareerEvent touches reputation — only those that carry verified professional weight, and they move it in both directions on one symmetric rule. A reputation event is any CareerEvent whose verification changes a dimension of standing: a publication lifts Research, a directorship lifts Leadership, a board certification lifts Authority and sets a floor. The same engine that records a rise records a fall — a license restriction or a substantiated investigation lowers standing, but only once verified at primary source, exactly as a positive event must be. The magnitude scales with selectivity and severity; the direction is set by the event class; and a pending, unproven allegation moves nothing at all.

One ledger, both directions · a rise and a fall pass through the identical verification gate — the engine is as disciplined about lowering standing as raising it, which is what makes a clean record mean something and an adverse one fair.

The symmetry rulea fall is as provable as a rise

Reputation moves down only on the same evidentiary bar it moves up. A negative event must be verified at primary source — a board action published, a finding substantiated — before it touches a single dimension. An accusation, a rumor, a complaint under review: none of these are facts yet, so the engine holds reputation steady until they become facts or are dismissed. This protects the innocent as rigorously as it records the proven.

What is excluded entirelyevents that look relevant but aren't

Volume without verification is not an event. Profile views, messages received, searches appeared-in, connections made — high-traffic signals a social platform would weight — are invisible to the engine. So are unverified self-reported achievements. A moment only becomes a reputation event when it is a dated, sourced, typed CareerEvent that verified; everything else is noise the engine never sees.

Deliverable · Reputation EventsA reputation event is a verified CareerEvent that changes a dimension of standing — publications, promotions, appointments, certifications, and conferred recognition raise it; substantiated investigations, license restrictions, and retractions lower it. Both directions pass the same primary-source gate, so a fall is exactly as provable as a rise, magnitude scales with selectivity and severity, and an unproven allegation moves nothing until it is proven.
D4

Reputation Visualization

standing without a social network

The hardest part of showing reputation is not the rendering — it is refusing every affordance that would turn it into a social network. The same data viewed three ways serves three readers: a clinician sees their own standing and how to strengthen it; a recruiter sees credibility weighted to a role; an employer sees dependability and any honest adverse history. But every view obeys the same constraints — no followers, no feed, no leaderboard, no public score ranked against named people. Every bar a reader sees dereferences to the verified evidence beneath it, and the only comparison ever drawn is against an anonymous field median, never against another clinician. Reputation is shown as a profile to be read, never a number to be ranked.

The anti-social-network principles · what the view refuses by design
No followers or connections
Reputation has no social graph. There is nothing to follow, no audience to grow, no connection count. Standing is computed from evidence, not from who you know or who watches you.
No feed or virality
There is no stream and nothing to broadcast. A reputation cannot be liked, shared, boosted, or go viral. It changes only when a CareerEvent verifies — silently, on the record, not in a timeline of posts.
No public leaderboard
No clinician is ranked against another by name. The only benchmark shown is an anonymous field median, and even that is contextual. Reputation answers "credible at what," never "who is winning."
No score without its evidence
Every dimension is traceable to source. Tap any bar and it resolves to the verified events that produced it. A standing you cannot audit is not shown — the projection is reversible, per canon, or it is not displayed at all.
Three lenses on one profile · what each reader sees
Clinician viewmy standing

A mirror and a path. The clinician sees their full seven-dimension profile, which dimensions are strong and which are thin, and — for each — the verified events that would strengthen it. It is private, actionable, and honest: it never flatters, and it shows the evidence beneath every bar so standing is legible, not mysterious.

Recruiter viewcredibility for this role

The profile, weighted to the opening. The recruiter sees the dimensions that matter for the role being filled foregrounded, with depth and recency surfaced. It reads the profile against the role's requirements — a gate, not a ranking — so two candidates are compared to the job, never crudely to each other.

Employer viewwill they perform & stay

Dependability, read honestly. The employer sees Operational, Clinical, and Authority standing foregrounded, consistency over time, and any adverse history surfaced plainly — neither hidden nor sensationalized. The question is steadiness and safety, answered from the same verified record everyone else reads.

Deliverable · Reputation VisualizationThree lenses — clinician, recruiter, employer — render one profile for three questions, under constraints that forbid every social-network affordance: no followers, no feed, no named leaderboard, and no score that does not dereference to its evidence. Reputation appears as a profile to be read and audited, compared only to an anonymous field median, never as a popularity figure ranked against named peers. The design refuses to be a network, and that refusal is the feature.
D5

Reputation Portability

standing that survives every change

A reputation an employer can erase by ending a job is not a reputation — it is a performance review. Real professional standing survives every change a career makes. Because reputation is a projection of the clinician-owned EvidenceObject ledger — never held by an employer, a state board, or a platform — it travels exactly as far as the evidence does, which is everywhere. When the world changes, the engine does not reset; it recomputes. Earned standing in transferable dimensions carries in full; standing tied to a specific context moves into history as a true past fact; and new context simply adds new evidence to project from. The ledger is portable, so the reputation is portable. That is the entire mechanism.

The three laws of reputation portability
01
Reputation is owned, never held.

It is a projection of the clinician's own ledger, not a record kept by an employer or a platform. reputation = project( owned_evidence ). No institution can withhold, reset, or delete it, because none of them ever held it.

02
Change recomputes, it does not reset.

When context changes, the engine re-projects the same evidence into the new situation. Transferable standing carries; context-bound standing ages into history. nothing earned is deleted — it is only ever re-read.

03
Portability is inherited from the ledger.

Reputation is exactly as portable as the EvidenceObject it projects from — which W215 made fully portable and W228 froze as canon. The engine adds no portability of its own; it simply cannot be less portable than its substrate.

Deliverable · Reputation PortabilityReputation survives every change because it is a projection of an owned, portable ledger — it recomputes, it never resets. An employer change carries the whole profile; a state change carries everything national; career and specialty changes carry the cross-cutting dimensions and age the context-bound ones into history. Nothing earned is ever deleted, because no employer, board, or platform ever held the reputation to take it away.

how reputation is built over a career

The five deliverables compose into one engine and answer one question: how does a professional build reputation over forty years? Not in a moment, and not by being noticed — but by accumulating verified facts and conferred recognition across seven dimensions, era by era, until standing is wide, deep, and impossible to fake. Reputation is the compounding interest of a career of proven work.
