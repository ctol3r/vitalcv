What VitalCV does — and what it explicitly does not.

Six reusable surfaces for naming the boundary between automated, source-backed work and manual administrative work. Honesty is the highest trust signal. The Chief Medical Officer who sees an explicit limitation trusts the data we do verify more than they would trust a tool that overclaims.

Component 01 · everywhere paragraph-style

Implementation note — what the platform does not automate.

A neutral, paragraph-style callout. Never red. Never amber. Never an icon. It is not a warning; it is a clarification. Used on overview surfaces (Employer Pipeline header, Source Health summary) to set expectations before a CMO reads the data.

ink-0 on ink-50
  • Lead with the noun — “State medical board PSV” not “We don't…”
  • Name what does happen — manual, asynchronous, vendor-channel, cohort-bound.
  • Never end with an apology — end with the operational fact.

Component 02 · pipeline rows inline pill

Manual-step indicator — provenance, not status.

Three pills make the per-row distinction explicit: PRIMARY (source-resolved automatically), MANUAL (administrative work outside the platform), ATTESTED (subject-attested, not yet verified). A CMO scanning a 47-row cohort can see the mix instantly. No color theater — just contour and weight.

  • Order in a row: strongest provenance first.
  • Total counts roll up to the cohort header, never aggregated as a percentage.

Component 03 · passport structured block

Passport scope notice — a public boundary statement.

Lives below the subject's verified data, never above it. Tells the verifier exactly which assertions the issuer is willing to back, and lists the assertions a sister system or human verifier must still cover. Reads as a contract clause, not as marketing.
Specificationscope-notice
Position
below subject record
Structure
2 lists · covered / not covered
Verb tense
present indicative
Voice
issuer, not subject
  • Covered items are verbatim from the receipt.
  • Not covered items name the responsible party.
  • No quantifiers (“mostly”, “generally”) — every line is binary.

Component 04 · missing integrations factual empty state

Awaiting integration — never “coming soon.”

Replaces the generic empty banner. Names the upstream registry, the access channel, and the cohort condition that would unlock it. A CMO who reads “awaiting integration” understands we know exactly what's missing — not that we forgot to build it.

A clinician-facing variant of the implementation note, embedded directly in the activation ritual. Calm, paragraph-form, no warning vocabulary. The clinician sees the boundary while their record is being assembled — so the limitation is contextual, not buried in the marketing site.

Component 06 · verifier replay field-level ledger

Field provenance — per-row source attribution.

For verifiers reading the replay. Every claim on the passport is tagged with its actual provenance and the registry it resolved from. A field marked MANUAL tells the verifier: this row needs your operations team's eyes before you accept the record.
