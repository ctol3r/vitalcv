If your CVO will rely on a VitalCV badge, this page is what to read first. No account needed.

A VitalCV "badge" is a small cryptographic document a clinician carries in their wallet. Anyone can check it without our help — and this page tells you who we are, what we signed, and how to verify it.

If you read nothing else VitalCV is a credential issuer, not a credentialing body. We attest that on the date stamped on a badge, we observed a clinician's source-of-truth records and bound them to that clinician's key. Whether that license is valid right now is a question the badge's status bit answers — checkable from your office in one HTTP call. Our server outage does not block your verification.

Who signs the badge

issuer identity · in plain English

The signer is VitalCV, Inc., identified by a decentralized identifier: a web address with a public key published at it. Verifiers fetch that key directly from vitalcv.health — not from a third-party directory we could quietly swap.

If you do not recognise the DID format, treat the right-hand block as the equivalent of a notary's seal: the address tells you whose seal it is, and the algorithm and fingerprint tell you which seal was used.

What this means for you: If the badge presented to your MSO does not resolve to this DID, it was not signed by us. Reject it.

What signed it

algorithm · public-key fingerprint

We sign with ed25519 — a modern, fast, widely supported algorithm. It is the same family of signatures used by SSH and most modern identity wallets. If your verifier library supports OpenID Connect, it supports this.

Below is the fingerprint of our current signing key — a four-block summary anyone in your office can read over the phone. If a colleague is staring at a different fingerprint, you are not looking at the same key.

We rotate this key on a 12-month schedule, with the next rotation publicly noted in advance. If we ever rotate off-schedule, it is because the previous key was retired for cause, and every badge it signed flips to revoked within 15 minutes.

How current is "verified"

freshness semantics · three distinct clocks

A badge says three different things about time. Reading them carefully prevents the most common mis-trust mistake — taking a signed credential as proof that everything it asserts is true right now.

Issuance time
When we signed it
The signature carries an exact UTC timestamp. It says: on this date we observed the source registries and they confirmed what the badge claims. The signature is permanent — it never goes stale even decades later.
on this badge2026-05-11T14:32:08Z
Status freshness
Whether it has been revoked
A separate file — the Bitstring Status List — flips a single bit if the underlying license is revoked. Your verifier should refetch the list at most every 15 minutes. A cached "valid" status older than that is stale.
status TTL≤ 15 minutes
Source re-resolution
Whether sources still agree
For high-stakes decisions you may want a live source recheck — we'll re-hit NPPES, OIG and the state board at the moment of your query and stamp the result. This is opt-in, takes ~2 seconds, and is the strongest read available.
live recheckverify.vitalcv.health?recheck=1
What freshness does not mean
"Verified" is never a statement that the clinician is currently practicing at your facility, or that their credentials are currently sufficient for the privileges you are granting. Those are decisions only your credentialing committee can make. A badge is an input; a privilege is a decision.

How to verify, in your office

three paths · choose by how much you want to trust us
Open the link the clinician sent you
The clinician presents a URL like verify.vitalcv.health/b/4f2a-7e08…. Opening it in any browser runs the full verification client-side. The page shows a one-line verdict and the artifacts above so you can sanity-check them.
Trust levelTrusts the browser only. Verification math runs locally in JS; the signature and status check don't pass through our server.
Or POST the badge to your own verifier
If you operate a CVO platform, run OID4VCI-compliant verification in your own stack. Fetch did:web:vitalcv.health, validate the signature against the JWKS, fetch the status list, check the bit. Total: four HTTP calls, all cacheable.
Trust levelTrusts nothing of ours but the math. We are entirely out of your verification path. If we are offline, your verification still completes.
Or request a live re-resolution
For the highest-confidence read, append ?recheck=1 to the verify URL. We re-hit NPPES, OIG, and the relevant state board at the moment of your query and stamp the result. Receipt is signed and added to the chain. ~2 seconds.
Trust levelTrusts current sources. Strongest available read. Use for unfamiliar issuers, high-risk privileges, or audit responses.
What you should not have to know
You are not expected to read the JSON-LD, parse the DID document, or write a Bitstring Status List decoder. Open the verify URL. The verdict line is the verdict. The artifacts above this page are auditable evidence — they exist so your security team can satisfy themselves, not so the credentialing committee has to learn cryptography.
