D1

Graph Projection Model

atom → graph element

Projection is the function project( evidenceObject ) → { node, properties[], edges[] } that turns an atom into structure. An EvidenceObject is one source-attributed assertion — a single observed fact. It does not become one thing in the graph; it becomes three at once. Its subject projects to a node, its attributes attach as properties, and its links — above all its provenance — emit relationships. The same atom is simultaneously a noun, the facts about that noun, and the connections it draws. Run projection over every object a career produces and the pile becomes a graph.

The three projection targets · every atom resolves into all three

Node — the subject

The durable thing the evidence is about projects to a node: a license, a position, a publication. A subject earns a node when it is referenceable by others and carries trust other nodes inherit. One node per distinct subject, content-addressed and never duplicated.

Property — the attributes

The evidence's own scalar fields — value, status, effective_period, tier, observed_at — attach to its node or edge as properties. A field projects to a property when it is an attribute of one thing, not independently referenceable. Properties describe; they never connect.

Relationship — the links

Whatever the evidence connects projects to a directed edge: who holds it, who issued it, who verified it. A claim projects to a relationship when it asserts a link between two nodes — especially provenance. The verification source is never a node; it is the edge that carries trust.

Becomes a node whendurable · shared · trust-bearing

The subject is a durable thing other clinicians can reference and that carries inheritable trust — a license, a degree, an organization, a specialty. If two careers could point at the same thing, it is a node.

Becomes a property whenscalar · local · descriptive

The fact is a scalar attribute of exactly one node or edge — a number, a date, a status. Properties never carry trust on their own and nothing else references them; they describe the thing they sit on.

Becomes a relationship whenlinks · directed · provenance

The assertion connects two nodes — who holds, who issued, who verified, who corroborates. Provenance always projects here: the source is the edge, which is why trust is a property of the path, not the claim.

Deliverable · Graph Projection Architectureproject( evidence ) = { node, properties[], edges[] }. The subject becomes a node, attributes become properties, links and provenance become edges. Projection is total — nothing in the evidence is dropped — and reversible — every graph element points back to the atom it came from. The graph is the index over evidence, materialized by running this one function over every object a career produces.
D2

Trust Topology

the shape of trust

Once evidence is projected, trust takes on a shape — a topology with an origin, a direction of travel, and places where it concentrates. Trust is not spread evenly across the graph: it is minted at a small, finite set of authorities, travels inward along credential edges to clinicians while attenuating with every hop, and pools at the shared anchors many clinicians connect to. Understanding the topology is what lets the network reuse a single verification across thousands of people — trust collects exactly where the graph is densest.

The three regions of the trust field
Trust topology · where it is born, how it moves, where it poolsfinite origins · attenuating travel · pooling anchors
Region 01
Origin
authority

Trust is minted, never claimed. It enters the graph only at authority nodes — state boards, ABMS, NPPES, NPDB, primary-source verifiers. This set is small and finite, so verifying each authority once unlocks every credential it will ever issue.

Region 02
Travel
source · T4 credential clinician attenuates with each hop

Trust flows inward, weighted by tier. It moves source → credential → clinician along directed edges. A T4 signed primary-source edge passes nearly all of it; each hop and each lower tier attenuates it. Distance from the authority is loss of trust.

Region 03
Compound
shared anchor

Trust pools at shared anchors. Where many clinicians connect to one node — a board, an employer, a specialty — trust becomes transitive. The 1,000th clinician on an anchor inherits the verification the 1st paid to establish. Density is where trust compounds.

The five laws of the topology
01
Trust has finite origins.

It is born only at authority nodes — a bounded set of boards, registries, and verifiers. No clinician, credential, or document originates trust; they can only carry what an authority minted. Securing the origins secures the whole field.

02
Trust travels inward and attenuates.

It flows from source toward clinician along directed edges, scaled by tier. T4 passes nearly all; T1 passes almost none. Every hop away from the authority is a loss — proximity to source is the measure of strength.

03
Independent paths multiply.

When two genuinely independent edges reach the same fact, confidence rises super-linearly — a corroborates edge is worth more than a second copy of the same source. Independence, not volume, is the scarce input that thickens trust.

04
Trust pools where the graph is dense.

Shared anchors concentrate it. The more clinicians and sources attach to a node, the more verification cost it amortizes and the more transitive trust it can lend. The network's value is highest exactly where its topology is densest.

05
The field is auditable end to end.

Because trust only ever travels along projected, source-bound edges, any value resolves to the exact path that produced it — origin, hops, tiers, receipts. The topology is a directed field with no opaque region; every point traces home.

Deliverable · Trust Propagation ModelTrust originates at finite authorities, travels inward along tiered edges while attenuating, multiplies under independent corroboration, and pools at the shared anchors many careers touch. That topology is why the network gets cheaper and stronger as it grows: every new clinician or source attached to an existing anchor deepens the pool the rest already draw from.

Graph Experiences

one graph, three first-views

The same graph opens to a different first screen depending on who walks in. A clinician, a recruiter, and an employer read one substrate, but each enters at a different node and needs a different first answer — so the design question is not "what can they see," it is "what should they see first." The first-view is the whole product in one frame: it must answer the reader's single most important question before they touch anything, and route them straight to what they do next. None of them re-collects evidence; they read what was projected once.

D4

Reputation Model

four layers, cleanly separated

Evidence, trust, recognition, and reputation are four distinct layers — and conflating them is the most common mistake in any professional profile. Evidence is what was observed; trust is what can be relied on; recognition is what peers chose to confer; reputation is what emerges when all of it is read together over a career. They form a ladder you cannot skip: recognition without evidence is empty, reputation without trust is reputation-laundering. The point of separating them is to show that reputation is never claimed or stored — it emerges from the layers below, which is exactly what makes it hard to fake.

How reputation emerges · three properties of the top rung
Property A

Emergent, not stored

Reputation is read, never written. It is not a node and not a score sitting in a field — it is recomputed from the whole subgraph on every look. There is nothing to edit, inflate, or set; you can only grow the evidence, trust, and recognition it emerges from.

Property B

Relative, read in a field

Reputation only means something against peers. A trajectory, a publication density, a leadership arc are read relative to a specialty and a cohort. The graph supplies the field — the shared anchors that let one career be read against the others on them.

Property C

Durable, hard to fake

Reputation is slow and composite. Because every input is source-bound and corroborated, it cannot be bought or asserted in a day. It moves at the speed of real evidence, which is exactly why a high-reputation subgraph is trustworthy — the cost to fake it is the cost to actually earn it.

Deliverable · Reputation ArchitectureEvidence is observed, trust is computed, recognition is conferred, reputation emerges. Keep the four layers separate and reputation stops being a vanity number and becomes what it should be — the emergent, relative, durable read of a real career. It cannot be claimed, only grown, because every rung beneath it is source-bound. That is how a profile earns the right to be believed.
05

Evidence → Network

the success criterion

The four deliverables compose into one answer. Projection turns atoms into structure, topology gives trust a shape, three readers share one substrate, and four layers separate so reputation can emerge. Read together they resolve the wave's question: how does evidence become a network?

Q1How does an EvidenceObject become a graph element?
By projection. project( evidence ) → { node, properties[], edges[] }. The subject becomes a node, its attributes become properties, and its links and provenance become relationships. One atom resolves into all three at once, totally and reversibly — the graph stores nothing the evidence does not already carry.
Q2Which evidence becomes a relationship, not a node?
Anything that asserts a link between two nodes — and provenance above all. The verification source never becomes a node you browse to; it becomes the verified_by edge that carries trust into the node. Trust is a property of the path, which is why edges, not subjects, are where the network lives.
Q3What is the topology of trust?
Trust is born at finite authorities, travels inward along tiered edges while attenuating with every hop, multiplies under independent corroboration, and pools at the shared anchors many careers touch. It is a directed, auditable field — densest, and most valuable, exactly where the graph is most shared.
Q4Why is it one product, not three?
Because all three readers traverse one projected substrate — they only differ in their door and their first question. Clinician enters at self and sees readiness; recruiter enters at an anchor and sees a ranked pipeline; employer enters at a role and sees fit with risk. No reader re-collects what another captured.
Q5How does reputation emerge?
From a ladder that cannot be skipped: evidence observed → trust computed → recognition conferred → reputation emerged. Reputation is read, not stored; relative to a peer field; and durable because every rung beneath it is source-bound. It is grown, never claimed.
Q6How does evidence become a network?
Project every atom onto shared anchors. The instant a node is shared, the verification one clinician paid for becomes reusable by everyone attached to the same node — and trust, projected along edges, reaches them all. Projection makes the molecule; shared anchors make it a lattice. Run projection over a population and the pile of evidence is a network.
DeterminationEvidence Objects are atoms; projection is the bond; shared anchors are the lattice. Place each atom into the graph as node, property, and edge; let trust take its topology of origin, travel, and compounding; open one substrate through three doors; and let reputation emerge from the four-layer ladder. Do this across a profession and evidence stops being a pile of records and becomes the network that remembers it.
