The argument
in one sentence

Every physician you hire spends ~100 days off the schedule while their licenses, registrations, and standing are re-verified against the same federal sources every other hospital already verified them against. The work is duplicative by design, and the cost is paid in revenue you will never recognize.

§01

Why healthcare onboarding is broken

three structural failures
Failure 01
Document re-collection

A US physician's professional identity is materially established by federal authorities of record — NPPES for identity, OIG/LEIE for sanctions, PECOS for Medicare enrollment, DEA Connect for controlled substances. Each authority returns a stable, machine-readable answer.

Hospitals do not consume those answers. They request fax-back copies of paper licenses, malpractice certificates, CV PDFs, and CME transcripts — an analog re-representation of digital truth that already exists. Each new employer re-collects the same packet from scratch.

Failure 02
Serial verification

Primary source verification, payer enrollment, practice privileging, and DEA reconciliation are run in series, not in parallel — because each step waits on a document that should have been a database lookup. Median time-to-first-claim is 103 days; nothing about the actual verification work justifies more than a fraction of that.

Failure 03
No reusable artifact

When the verification finally completes, the result is filed inside a credentialing platform that the next hospital cannot read. There is no portable, signed, replayable artifact — so the next time a payer, a privileging committee, or a credentials verification organization needs the same answer, the chain starts again from zero.

§03

Why reusable verification matters

three properties · structural
01

Source-direct, not source-of-record.

VitalCV reads from federal authorities of record at run time. We do not stage a copy and ask hospitals to trust ours. The audit row records what the registry returned, signed at the moment we asked, replayable for seven years.

02

Subject-bound, not employer-owned.

The credential is bound to the clinician's holder key, not the issuing hospital. When the physician moves, the credential moves with them. The next employer does not re-collect. They re-verify the existing signature.

03

Replayable, not promissory.

Every verification produces a signed receipt with checkedAt, runId, lineageKey, ownership attestation, and an event chain hash. A downstream auditor can re-derive the proof offline without calling our infrastructure. The artifact is the trust.

§04

What VitalCV is not

explicit limits · institutional maturity
Boundary
What stays manual

VitalCV does not replace the human credentialing function. State board outreach to non-API jurisdictions, malpractice carrier confirmations, hospital committee scheduling, reference letter chase, DEA modification filings — these remain manual by design. We replace the busywork around credentialing, not the judgment inside it.

Anything that requires negotiation, scheduling, or non-API human contact stays where the relationships already live: with your CVO, your committee chairs, and your payer reps. We surface state. We write the audit row when the human work completes.
