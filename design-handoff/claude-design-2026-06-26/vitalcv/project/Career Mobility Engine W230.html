D1

Mobility Doctrine

what career mobility is

Career mobility is the rate at which a clinician can move to a more valuable position — and be believed on arrival. Every mobility event is the same shape: a clinician crosses a threshold into a new role — new employment, a leadership promotion, a faculty appointment, medical-staff privileges, a locums assignment, a research seat, a board appointment. Each crossing has a gate: a set of trust dimensions and evidence the destination requires. Mobility is high when a career clears those gates with verification already in hand and low when it must stop to re-prove itself. The doctrine names the three forces that govern every crossing — what creates mobility, what blocks it, and what accelerates it.

The three forces of mobility
What creates · what blocks · what accelerates a career crossingtrust opens · gaps close · proof pre-clears
Force 01
Creates
verified trust opens role

Verified trust that clears a gate. Mobility is created when a career's trust dimensions already satisfy what a role requires — the right authority, current standing, corroborated evidence. The opportunity opens because the proof is in hand, not promised. Trust is the key; the role is the door.

Force 02
Blocks
career role missing gate

A missing or stale gate. Mobility is blocked by a gap the role demands and the career cannot show — missing authority, missing experience, missing or expired evidence. A single unmet gate stops the crossing entirely; advancement is gated by its weakest required dimension, not its average.

Force 03
Accelerates
pre-cleared proof fast lane

Pre-cleared, portable proof. Mobility accelerates when verification is reusable — corroborated, current, and already legible to the destination's credentialing system. The work was done once; every subsequent crossing skips the line. Portability turns months of re-proving into days.

The four laws of mobility
01
Mobility is gated, not scored.

A crossing succeeds or fails on whether specific required gates are met — not on a high overall average. A brilliant record with one missing authority does not "mostly" cross; it is blocked. The engine reasons in gates met and unmet, never in a single number.

02
The weakest required gate governs.

Advancement is limited by the lowest-cleared dimension the destination demands. mobility = min(required gates). Strength elsewhere cannot compensate for an unmet requirement — you cross at the level of your weakest gate, not your strongest credential.

03
Portability is the multiplier.

The same verified evidence, reused, accelerates every future crossing. A career whose proof is corroborated and current moves at a fraction of the cost of one that must re-verify each time. Mobility compounds with how reusable a record already is.

04
The engine advances, never invents.

Mobility is computed only from earned evidence. The engine can match a career to an opportunity and name the exact gap, but it cannot conjure the missing credential — it shows the path and the price, and the clinician walks it.

Deliverable · Mobility ArchitectureCareer mobility is the rate at which verified trust clears the gates of more valuable roles. It is created by trust that already satisfies a gate, blocked by any unmet gate, and accelerated by portable, pre-cleared proof. Every mobility event is a gated crossing; the engine's job is to know each gate, read each career against it, and route the clinician to the opening they can actually cross — or to the gap they must close first.
D2

OpportunityObject

the opening as a structured object

If a career is a graph of evidence, an opportunity must be an object of requirements — symmetric, so the two can be compared directly. The OpportunityObject is the invented primitive of this wave: a structured, machine-readable description of any opening that states exactly what it demands in the same vocabulary a VitalCV career is built from — dimensions, evidence, trust tiers. Where the EvidenceObject (W215) is one observed fact, the OpportunityObject is one required set of facts. Representing openings this way is what lets the engine compare any role to any career without a human re-reading either: requirements meet evidence, thresholds meet dimensions, and a verdict falls out.

Symmetry is the point · the opportunity speaks the career's own vocabulary, so comparison is mechanical, not editorial — one missing reference is the entire distance between this career and this role.

How opportunities are representedsymmetric to evidence

As a structured object in the same vocabulary as a career — dimensions, evidence, tiers, currency. An opening stops being prose a human interprets and becomes a gate a machine can test a record against, identically every time.

How they compare to careersrequirements ↔ evidence

By projecting requirements onto the career graph and resolving each: met, partial, or gap. Thresholds test dimensions; evidence requirements test artifacts; readiness tests currency. The verdict is the set of unmet gates, named precisely.

Why gates beat signalshard floors vs soft sort

Thresholds and evidence are hard gates — fail one and you cannot cross. Mobility signals are soft: they rank candidates who already clear the gates. Separating the two keeps fit honest and stops a strong "signal" from masking a missing requirement.

Deliverable · Opportunity ModelThe OpportunityObject represents any opening as five property groups — requirements, trust thresholds, evidence requirements, readiness requirements, and mobility signals — in the same vocabulary a career is built from. Because opportunity and career are symmetric, matching is a mechanical projection of one onto the other: gates resolve to met, partial, or gap, and the distance to a role becomes an exact, addressable list.
D3

Career Gap Model

naming the distance to a role

The gap engine turns a comparison into a verdict a clinician can act on. Matching a career to an OpportunityObject produces a set of resolved gates; the gap engine reads that set and assigns a single, honest state — and, when a gap exists, names exactly what kind it is. The crucial design choice is that "not ready" is never a dead end: the engine distinguishes missing evidence (you have it, prove it) from missing experience (you must do it) from missing authority (you must earn it), because each implies a completely different next action and a completely different timeline. A gap that is named is a gap that can be closed.

Career gap framework · the six states
Six states · from cleared to blocked, each with its resolutionstate = f( resolved gates )
Readystate · cross now
Every required gate is met and current. The career clears the opportunity today with verification already in hand.
Resolution · apply / pre-clear — nothing stands in the way.
Near Readystate · days away
All gates met, but one is decaying or pending a refresh — a stale read, a reference in flight. Closeable fast.
Resolution · re-verify the stale credential; chase the pending item.
Missing Evidencegap · prove it
The career likely has the qualification but has not verified it — an unproven license, an uncorroborated role.
Resolution · collect a primary-source read. Timeline: days.
Missing Experiencegap · do it
A required tenure, volume, or role has not yet been lived — years as attending, case count, a leadership term.
Resolution · accrue it on a pathway. Timeline: months–years.
Missing Authoritygap · earn it
A credential or legal standing does not exist — an unheld license, an unearned board certification, no privileges.
Resolution · sit the exam / obtain the license. Timeline: months.
Blockedstate · disqualified
A hard disqualifier is present — an open board action, an NPDB flag, an unexplained gap the role forbids.
Resolution · resolve the action first; no other gate matters until then.

Same career, four verdicts · the state is a property of the pairing, not the person — Dr. A is Ready for one role and months away from another, and the engine says precisely which and why.

Why name the gap typeaction, not verdict

"Not ready" is useless; "missing one reference, 3 days" is a plan. Naming whether a gap is evidence, experience, or authority converts a rejection into a next step with a timeline — the difference between a wall and a door.

Why Blocked is separatea gate before the gates

A disqualifier is not a gap to fill — it is a condition that voids every other gate. The engine surfaces it first and alone, because no amount of evidence or experience matters while an open action stands.

Why state is per-pairingrelative, never absolute

A career is never "ready" in the abstract — only ready for a specific opportunity. The same record is Ready, Near, or Blocked depending on the gate it is read against, which is why the engine always evaluates the pair, not the person.

Deliverable · Career Gap FrameworkThe gap engine assigns one of six states — Ready, Near Ready, Missing Evidence, Missing Experience, Missing Authority, Blocked — to each career-opportunity pairing, and names the deciding gap. By separating gaps you can prove from gaps you must live and gaps you must earn, it turns "no" into a dated, addressable path — and keeps a hard disqualifier from hiding behind an otherwise strong record.
D4

Career Pathways

how growth is modeled

Individual crossings add up to a trajectory — and VitalCV models that trajectory as a pathway: a sequence of roles connected by the gates between them. A pathway is not a prediction or a prescription; it is the structure of progression a specialty already contains, made explicit. Resident becomes attending; attending becomes medical director; clinician branches to faculty or to researcher. Each transition is an OpportunityObject gate, so a pathway is just gates chained end to end — which means the gap engine can run against a destination several steps away and tell a clinician not only their next move but the whole staircase, and exactly which step they are standing on.

A pathway is addressable · run the gap engine against a node two steps ahead and it returns the gates at each rung — the clinician sees the whole staircase, not just the next step.

How VitalCV models growth · three properties of a pathway
Property A

Descriptive, not prescriptive

A pathway maps what exists, it does not dictate. VitalCV reads the gates a specialty already imposes and makes them legible; it never tells a clinician they should become a director. The structure is the field's, surfaced — the choice of route stays entirely the clinician's.

Property B

Branching, not linear

Growth forks. A clinician can branch toward leadership, academia, or research, and the same evidence carries different weight on each branch. Modeling growth as a graph of gates — not a single ladder — lets the engine show every viable direction, with its distinct gate at each fork.

Property C

Computed from the same gates

Pathways reuse the OpportunityObject. Nothing new is invented — each transition is the same gated crossing as any single opportunity. That is why the gap engine runs unchanged against a destination three rungs away, returning a dated plan for the whole climb, not just the next door.

Deliverable · Career Pathway ArchitectureVitalCV models growth as pathways — sequences of roles connected by OpportunityObject gates, branching rather than linear, descriptive rather than prescriptive. Because every transition reuses the same gate machinery, the gap engine can evaluate any destination on the pathway and return the full staircase: where the clinician stands, what each next rung requires, and how far each branch reaches.
D5

Opportunity Matching

evidence against requirements

Matching is the engine's output — the moment career evidence and opportunity requirements resolve into a decision. It is not one verdict but three, on different time horizons. Fit asks whether the career is the right shape for the role at all. Readiness asks whether it can cross the gate today. Future readiness asks how far away it is if not, and on which pathway. Separating the three is what makes the match useful to everyone at once: a recruiter sees fit, a credentialer sees readiness, and the clinician sees the path to future readiness — all from the same projection of evidence onto requirements.

Mobility matching framework · three horizons of a match

Fit — right shape?

Whether the career's dimensions and trajectory align with the role's shape — specialty, setting, signals. Fit is about direction, not currency: a strong interventional cardiologist fits a cath-lab role even if a credential is momentarily stale. Fit answers "is this the right kind of career for this opening?"

Readiness — cross today?

Whether every hard gate is met and current right now. Readiness is binary and unforgiving — one unmet threshold or stale read drops it. It answers "can this clinician be credentialed into this role this week?" and is exactly the Ready / Near / Blocked state from the gap engine.

Future readiness — how far?

If not ready, the distance and the pathway to becoming ready — which gaps, what kind, how long. Future readiness turns a "no" into a trajectory: collect one reference (days), accrue tenure (years). It answers "if not now, when, and by what route?"

What creates fitshape & direction

Alignment of the career's dimensions and trajectory with the role's shape. Fit is generous and directional — it tolerates a stale credential because it asks whether the career is pointed the right way, not whether it is cleared today.

What creates readinessevery gate, now

All hard gates met and current. Readiness is strict and present-tense: verified evidence, cleared thresholds, no stale reads, no disqualifiers. It is the difference between a good candidate and one who can start.

What creates future readinessa closeable path

Named gaps on a real pathway, each with a timeline. Future readiness exists when every gap is the kind that can be closed — evidence, experience, or authority — and the engine can route the clinician to close it.

Deliverable · Mobility Matching FrameworkMatching projects evidence onto requirements and returns three horizons — fit (right shape), readiness (cross today), and future readiness (the dated path if not). One projection serves every reader: the recruiter ranks on fit, the credentialer acts on readiness, the clinician follows future readiness. That is the engine's whole purpose realized — trust, resolved into a decision and a next step.
06

Trust → Opportunity

the success criteria

The five deliverables compose into one engine. The doctrine defines mobility as gated crossings; the OpportunityObject makes openings comparable; the gap engine names the distance; pathways chain the gates into a trajectory; and matching resolves it all into fit, readiness, and future readiness. Read together they answer the wave's question: how does trust become opportunity?

Q1What is career mobility?
The rate at which verified trust clears the gates of more valuable roles — and the clinician is believed on arrival. Every mobility event, from new employment to a board appointment, is a gated crossing; mobility is high when a career meets the gate with proof in hand and low when it must stop to re-prove itself.
Q2What creates opportunity?
A representable opening — the OpportunityObject — meeting a career that clears its gates. Opportunity is created when an opening's trust thresholds, evidence, and readiness requirements are all satisfied by evidence the career has already earned. The object makes the opening comparable; the cleared gate makes it real.
Q3What creates readiness?
Every hard gate met and current, with no disqualifier. Readiness is strict and present-tense — verified evidence, cleared thresholds, fresh reads. It is the gap engine's Ready state: not the right shape (that is fit) and not a closeable path (that is future readiness), but the ability to be credentialed in today.
Q4What blocks advancement?
Any unmet required gate — and a disqualifier above all. Because mobility = min(required gates), a single missing authority, missing experience, or stale evidence stops a crossing regardless of strength elsewhere. A hard disqualifier — an open action, an NPDB flag — voids every other gate until it is resolved.
Q5How does trust become opportunity?
By projection and matching. The same verified trust dimensions that compounded in W222 are projected onto an OpportunityObject's gates; where they clear, the opportunity opens; where they fall short, the gap engine names the exact, dated distance. Trust becomes opportunity the instant it is read against a gate it satisfies.
Q6How does VitalCV help a clinician grow over a career?
By making the whole staircase visible and addressable. It models progression as branching pathways of chained gates, runs the gap engine against any destination, and returns fit, readiness, and future readiness — so a clinician always sees where they stand, what the next rung costs, and which branch reaches furthest. VitalCV stops measuring the career and starts advancing it.
DeterminationMobility is a gated crossing; the OpportunityObject is the gate; the gap engine is the distance; pathways are the staircase; matching is the decision. Project a career's earned trust onto an opening's requirements and opportunity falls out — named, dated, and addressable. Run it across a career and verified evidence stops being a record of where a clinician has been and becomes the engine of where they can go next.
