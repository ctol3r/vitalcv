D1

Trust Origins

where trust is minted

Trust is minted, not claimed. It enters the graph only when an authority performs a primary-source verification — a state board confirming a license, ABMS confirming a certification, an institution attesting employment. Everything else only references trust that was minted somewhere else: a CV line, a self-reported skill, a copied credential number. The single most important distinction in the engine is therefore between an origin (creates trust) and a reference (points at trust). And both differ from evidence: evidence is the observed assertion; trust is the reliance that a verified origin lets you place on it. Evidence is what was seen — trust is what an authority will stand behind.

Three things people confuse · origin vs reference vs evidence

Origin — creates trust

A primary-source verification at an authority. The board, the registry, the issuer confirms a fact against its own system of record. This act mints trust into the graph — it is the only thing that does. A finite set of origins, each verified once, backs every credential they will ever issue.

Reference — points at trust

A claim that names a credential without verifying it — a CV entry, a self-report, a screenshot, a number typed into a form. A reference carries zero trust of its own; it inherits only what its origin minted, and only once that origin is reached. Unbacked, it is just an assertion awaiting verification.

Evidence — what was observed

The source-bound assertion itself — the W215 atom. Evidence is the raw observation: "this license exists, status active, read on this date." Trust is a separate layer computed from evidence and its origin's tier. Evidence can exist with no trust; trust can never exist without evidence.

Creates trust whenauthority · primary-source · signed

An authority verifies a fact against its own system of record and signs the read. Only this mints trust. The set of authorities is finite, so each is verified once and backs every credential it issues forever — the origin, not the claim, is what the engine trusts.

Only references trust whennames · copies · asserts

A record names a credential without reaching its authority — a CV line, a badge, a typed number. It carries no trust of its own and inherits its origin's trust only once that origin is read. A million references to an unverified claim still sum to zero trust.

Is evidence, not trust, whenobserved · source-bound · pre-reliance

The item is an observation awaiting reliance. Evidence is the fact that was seen; trust is what an origin's tier lets you place on it. Evidence with a weak or missing origin stays evidence — present, recorded, but not yet something anyone should rely on.

Deliverable · Trust Origin ArchitectureTrust is minted only by primary-source verification at a finite set of authorities; everything else references trust or is merely evidence. An origin creates; a reference inherits; evidence is the raw observation beneath both. Separating the three is what lets the engine ignore the volume of claims and trust only the verifications — and verify each origin exactly once.
D2

Trust Propagation Model

how trust flows into dimensions

Minting creates trust at a point; propagation decides where it goes. A verified origin does not raise some global score — it increments a specific trust dimension and nothing else. A Board Certification increases Professional Authority; a Publication increases Research Trust; a Faculty Appointment increases Academic Trust. Propagation is the directed routing of minted trust from an origin to the one or two dimensions it actually evidences, scaled by the origin's tier and the directness of the path. The three design questions are how far trust should travel, what weakens it, and what amplifies it — and the answers keep the engine honest: trust reaches only what an origin can truly speak to.

The three propagation questions, answered as a field
How far it travels · what weakens it · what amplifies itbounded reach · attenuation · corroboration
Question 01
How far it travels
origin reach limit · 2–3 hops

As far as relevance, then it stops. Trust travels one hop to the dimension an origin evidences, and at most one or two further along genuine relationships — holder, issuer, employer. Beyond that the link is too weak to carry meaning, so reach is bounded by design, not distance.

Question 02
What weakens it
T4 source distant claim −tier · −hop · −time

Distance, low tier, and age. Every hop from the origin attenuates trust; a lower-tier path passes less of it; and time decays it (D4). A self-reported, third-hand, year-old claim weakens on all three axes at once — what reaches the dimension is a fraction of what was minted.

Question 03
What amplifies it
independent sources converge · super-linear

Independent corroboration. When two genuinely independent origins reach the same fact, trust rises faster than the sum — a corroborates edge is worth more than a second copy. Recency amplifies too: a fresh primary-source read restores a dimension toward its minted ceiling.

The five laws of propagation
01
Trust routes; it does not flood.

An origin increments only the dimension it evidences. A board certification raises Professional Authority and stops; it never leaks into Research or Identity. Relevance, not magnitude, decides where minted trust is allowed to land.

02
Reach is bounded to a few hops.

Trust propagates one hop to its dimension and at most one or two further along real relationships. reach ≤ 3. Past that, the path no longer evidences anything, so the engine refuses to carry trust there at all.

03
Every hop and tier attenuates.

What arrives is what was minted scaled by tier and discounted per hop. T4 direct passes nearly all; T1 third-hand passes almost none. Strength is proximity to a high-tier origin.

04
Independence amplifies super-linearly.

Two independent origins on one fact are worth more than twice one. Volume of copies adds nothing; corroborates from a distinct authority is the scarce input that pushes a dimension toward its ceiling.

05
Propagation is fully auditable.

Because trust only moves along projected, source-bound edges, any dimension value resolves to the exact origins, tiers, and hops that produced it. There is no propagation the graph cannot show its work for.

Deliverable · Trust Propagation FrameworkA verified origin increments a specific dimension, propagates at most two to three hops along real relationships, attenuates with every hop and lower tier, and amplifies only under independent corroboration. Trust is routed, not broadcast — which is exactly why a clinician strong in one dimension is not automatically trusted in another, and why the network cannot be gamed by volume.
D3

Trust Dimensions

many scores, not one

Trust is not one number — it is seven. A single score is a lie of compression: it lets a brilliant researcher with a lapsed license look "trusted," and a flawless community clinician with no publications look "untrusted." A profession does not work that way. Someone is trusted for specific things — that they are who they say, that they hold real authority, that they practice well, that their research holds, that they lead, that institutions vouch for them, that they can move. Each is fed by different origins, decays at a different rate, and matters to a different reader. The engine keeps them separate and independently computed, and composes a headline only at the moment of reading, against a role.

Why not one scorecompression hides risk

A single number averages away the thing a reader needs. A high composite can hide a lapsed license; a low one can bury elite research. The dangerous facts live in the spread between dimensions — collapsing them deletes exactly the signal that matters.

Why seven, not infinitedistinct reader questions

Each dimension answers a question a real reader actually asks — identity for credentialing, authority for compliance, research for academia, mobility for recruiting. Fewer would conflate them; more would split hairs no decision turns on.

Where the one number livescomposed at read time

A headline is still useful — but it is computed against a role at the moment of reading, weighting the dimensions that role cares about. The seven are the truth stored; the single score is a view, never the substrate.

Deliverable · Trust Dimension ModelTrust is seven independent dimensions — Identity, Authority, Professional, Research, Leadership, Institutional, Mobility — each fed by distinct origins and decaying at distinct rates, never a single score. One number is allowed only as a read-time projection weighted by the reader's role. Keeping the dimensions separate is what lets the same record be read correctly by a credentialer, an academic, and a recruiter at once.
D4

Trust Decay

trust has a half-life

Trust is current, not permanent — every dimension decays unless its evidence is refreshed. An expired license, a retired certification, an inactive practice, an employment three years stale: each was once a valid mint, but the world moved and the verification did not. Decay is the engine's negative half — it ensures a value reflects today's reliability, not the day it was last checked. Different evidence decays at different rates: an active license is volatile and must be re-read often; a publication is durable and barely fades; a degree never expires. The design is a half-life per evidence type, a trigger that can collapse it instantly, and a floor below which it does not fall.

The four laws of decay
01
Trust fades from the moment it is read.

A verification is a snapshot, and the clock starts immediately. Every dimension carries a read_at, and value is discounted by age against the evidence's half-life. There is no such thing as a permanently current trust value — only a recently refreshed one.

02
Volatility sets the half-life, not importance.

What decays fast is what can change fast — licenses, sanctions, privileges — regardless of how important it is. Durable facts that cannot un-happen barely decay. The rate tracks the evidence's capacity to go stale, nothing else.

03
Some events collapse trust instantly.

Expiry, suspension, board action, retraction don't decay gradually — they zero or step down the affected dimension on the next read. Decay is the slow path; a trigger is the cliff. The engine models both.

04
A fresh read restores toward the ceiling.

Decay is fully reversible by re-verification. Re-reading a volatile credential snaps its dimension back up; that is why the engine schedules refreshes for exactly the evidence whose half-life is shortest. Currency is maintained, not assumed.

Deliverable · Trust Decay FrameworkEvery dimension decays from its read date at a half-life set by how volatile its evidence is, can be collapsed instantly by an expiry or sanction, holds a floor that depends on whether the fact can un-happen, and is restored by a fresh primary-source read. Decay is what makes a VitalCV trust value mean "reliable today," not "was reliable once" — and it tells the engine precisely what to go re-verify.
D5

Trust Compounding

trust → reputation → mobility

Trust, accumulated and kept current, compounds — and that is where it turns into value. A clinician becomes more trusted not by asserting more but by accumulating verified, corroborated, recent evidence across dimensions over time. Sustained trust reads as reputation — the emergent standing of a whole career subgraph. Reputation, made portable and legible, becomes mobility — the ability to enter a new system, credential fast, and be believed on arrival. This is the engine's compounding loop: each verified credential lowers the cost and raises the speed of the next, so a strong record gets easier to extend, not harder.

The three growth mechanics · how the loop actually compounds
Mechanic A

A clinician becomes more trusted

By accumulating verified evidence, not asserting more. Each new primary-source credential, each independent corroboration, each fresh re-read raises a dimension and resets its decay. Breadth across dimensions plus recency plus corroboration is the only path up — and it is one nobody can shortcut.

Mechanic B

Trust becomes reputation

When sustained trust is read across the whole subgraph. Reputation is not a new score to earn; it emerges when high, current, corroborated dimensions persist over years and read well against a peer field. Time is the ingredient that cannot be bought — durability is what separates reputation from a good week.

Mechanic C

Reputation becomes mobility

When reputation is made portable and legible. A strong, current, corroborated subgraph clears a new credentialing system in days, not months — the verification was already paid for. High reputation is low switching cost; that is the value the whole engine exists to produce.

Deliverable · Trust Compounding ArchitectureA clinician grows trust by accumulating verified, corroborated, current evidence; sustained trust emerges as reputation; portable reputation realizes as mobility — and mobility produces new evidence, closing the loop. Each turn lowers the marginal cost of the next, so trust does not merely add up — it compounds into a career that is faster and cheaper to extend the more of it exists.
06

Trust → Value

the success criteria

The five deliverables compose into one engine. Origins mint trust, propagation routes it into dimensions, dimensions keep it honest, decay keeps it current, and compounding turns accumulated, sustained trust into reputation and mobility. Read together they resolve the wave's question: how does the graph produce value over time?

Q1What creates trust?
Primary-source verification at a finite set of authorities — and nothing else. An origin mints trust by confirming a fact against its own system of record and signing the read. A CV line, a badge, a self-report only reference trust; they inherit an origin's tier once it is reached and carry none of their own. The mint, not the claim, is what the engine trusts.
Q2How does trust move?
By routed propagation. A verified origin increments only the dimension it evidences — Board Cert → Professional Authority, Publication → Research — travels at most two to three hops along real relationships, attenuates with every hop and lower tier, and amplifies only under independent corroboration. Trust is directed, not broadcast, so strength in one dimension never leaks into another.
Q3How does trust decay?
From the moment it is read, at a half-life set by how volatile its evidence is. Volatile standing (licenses, privileges) fades in weeks and can be collapsed instantly by an expiry or sanction; periodic facts age out to a low floor; durable facts barely move and hold a high floor forever. A fresh read restores the dimension — so a value always means "reliable today."
Q4How does trust compound?
By accumulation across dimensions over time. Each verified, independent, recent credential raises a dimension and resets its decay, and breadth plus recency plus corroboration push the record toward its ceiling. Because shared anchors amortize verification, each new credential lowers the cost of the next — trust adds up super-linearly, not linearly.
Q5How does trust become reputation?
When sustained, current, corroborated trust is read across the whole subgraph against a peer field. Reputation is not minted or stored — it emerges from the dimensions below and is durable precisely because time and source-binding cannot be shortcut. A high-reputation record is one whose every input was actually earned.
Q6How does trust become mobility?
When reputation is made portable and legible. A strong, current, corroborated subgraph clears a new credentialing system in days because the verification was already paid for — high reputation is low switching cost. Mobility then produces new verified positions, which become new trust, closing the loop. That is the value the engine exists to create.
DeterminationTrust is minted at origins, routed into dimensions, kept current by decay, and compounded into reputation and mobility. The graph explained relationships; this engine explains value. Run it over a career and verified evidence stops being a static record and becomes a compounding asset — one that is most trustworthy, and most mobile, exactly because every gain in it had to be genuinely earned.
