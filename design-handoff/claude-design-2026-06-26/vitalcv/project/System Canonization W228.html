VitalCV / Canon Layer / W228 · System Canonization

Thirteen waves built an Evidence Network. Before it expands, one wave audits it — and asks the only question that matters: is the foundation correct?

This is not a build wave. It adds no object, no surface, no engine. Its entire job is judgment: to take the architecture that W215 through W230 assembled — Evidence, Graph, Trust, Timeline — hold it to the light, and decide whether it is coherent enough to deserve a decade of expansion on top of it. The audit does four things. It tests each layer for a unique purpose, redundancy, misplacement, and gaps. It freezes the ontology — five concepts, each ruled atom, aggregate, or projection, and each marked doctrine or not. It names the entities that must survive to Wave 300 and demotes the ones that should not. And it stress-tests readiness: can Career Wallet, Mobility, Matching, Reputation, and Network Effects ship on this substrate without a redesign? One finding governs all four — the system has exactly one store and everything else is a projection of it, and that is precisely what makes it correct.

D1

Ecosystem Audit

four layers, one test

The Evidence Network is four layers stacked on one atom — and the audit's first job is to confirm the stack does not repeat itself. Evidence (W215) invents the EvidenceObject, the source-bound atom. Graph (W220–W221) places atoms into structure via project(). Trust (W222) computes belief over that structure. Timeline (W225) reads the same record across time. The decisive observation is that only the first layer stores anything: Graph, Trust, and Timeline are functions of the EvidenceObject ledger, recomputable and reversible. That is the source of the architecture's coherence — and the lens through which every redundancy, misplacement, and gap below is judged.

Naming onlyNo structural redundancy — but two naming drifts that must be reconciled. "Professional Memory" is defined twice: as eleven evidence classes in W215 and as five memory layers in W225. And trust dimensions drifted from six (W220) to seven (W222). Neither stores a fact twice; both are vocabulary that diverged across waves and should be frozen to one definition each.

D2

Canonical Ontology

freeze the five concepts

An ontology becomes doctrine the moment its terms stop drifting — so the audit freezes five concepts and rules each one's kind. The waves produced five load-bearing nouns: EvidenceObject, CareerEvent, GraphNode, TrustProjection, TimelineProjection. The freeze sorts them into exactly three kinds — an atom that is stored, an aggregate that is composed, and a projection that is computed and never stored — and rules each as permanent doctrine or merely canonical. The point of the exercise is the single law that falls out of it: store once, project many; never store a projection.

The three laws of the frozen ontology
01
One store, never two.

Only the EvidenceObject is written. store = { EvidenceObject }. Graph, Trust, Timeline, and Mobility persist nothing of their own — if a fact appears to live in two places, one of them is a projection that must be recomputed, not saved.

02
Projections are pure and reversible.

Every node, trust value, and timeline cell dereferences to the exact evidence it came from. project⁻¹( x ) → evidence always resolves. A projection that cannot be traced back to source is a bug, not a feature.

03
An aggregate composes, it does not duplicate.

A CareerEvent is a named bundle of EvidenceObjects, not a copy of them. CareerEvent = ⋃ evidence. It carries no fact the atoms do not already hold — it only gives a set of them a date, a type, and a name.

Deliverable · Canonical OntologyFive concepts, three kinds: one atom (EvidenceObject), one aggregate (CareerEvent), three projections (GraphNode, TrustProjection, TimelineProjection). Four are permanent doctrine; CareerEvent is canonical but demoted from atom to aggregate. The doctrine they collapse into is a single law — store once, project many; never store a projection — and that law is itself the most permanent thing the wave produces.

The candidates: Clinician, Organization, Opportunity, Recognition, Credential, Evidence, Trust, CareerEvent. The test is brutal and simple — does this need to be a thing of its own, or is it already something we canonized? Three survive as subjects the network is built around, two survive as the atom and aggregate from D2, one survives only as a projection, and two fold into evidence and graph entirely. Fewer entities, sharper canon.

Network Readiness Review

Five products are on the roadmap: Career Wallet, Mobility Engine, Opportunity Matching, Professional Reputation, and Network Effects. Each is checked against one bar — is it native to the substrate (already expressible as a projection of the ledger), or does it need new build? And critically: of the things that need building, does any of them require the foundation itself to change? The answer decides the wave.

05

The Verdict

have we built the correct foundation?

The four deliverables converge on one judgment. The layers are orthogonal and non-redundant; the ontology freezes to one atom, one aggregate, and a family of projections; six entities survive to Wave 300 and two correctly fold away; and the roadmap ships on the substrate without a redesign. Read together they answer the wave's six questions — and the one question those six were always asking.

Q1Does each layer have a unique purpose?
Yes — and that is the architecture's whole strength. Evidence stores the fact, Graph connects it, Trust weighs it, Timeline times it. Four orthogonal axes of one record. The decisive property is that only Evidence stores; Graph, Trust, and Timeline are pure projections — which is exactly why they can never contradict each other.
Q2Is anything redundant?
No fact is stored twice — only two names drifted. "Professional Memory" was defined as eleven classes and again as five layers; trust dimensions slid from six to seven. Both are vocabulary, not structure. The fix is to freeze one definition each, which D2 does — there is no redundant store to remove.
Q3Is anything misplaced?
Two things, both re-classed not rebuilt. CareerEvent was billed as a second atom; it is an aggregate of evidence and moves up one level. Recognition was treated as a primitive; it is a conferred EvidenceObject and folds into Evidence. Correcting placement costs a definition, not a migration.
Q4Is anything missing?
Two additive pieces. Organization must be promoted from anchor to full subject so the network has a second pole, and the cross-career transitivity engine must be written so trust can flow between careers. Both extend the substrate; neither touches the atom. Missing is not the same as wrong.
Q5Which concepts are permanent doctrine?
The EvidenceObject and the projection law. One store, append-only, clinician-owned — and the rule that everything else is computed from it: store once, project many; never store a projection. GraphNode, TrustProjection, and TimelineProjection are doctrine because they encode that law in their very names.
Q6Have we built the correct foundation?
Yes. The system has exactly one store and reads it through many lenses; nothing on the roadmap forces a return to the atom to change it. The foundation is not just adequate — it is the kind you spend a decade building on. It earns its expansion. VitalCV stops asking whether the architecture holds and starts building the network it was always for.
Determination · W228 · System Canonizationthe foundation is correct — expand it

One store, many projections. The Evidence Network is coherent, and it deserves the decade that follows.

Thirteen waves produced an architecture with a single immutable atom, one append-only ledger, and a law that makes Graph, Trust, Timeline, and Mobility all reversible projections of it. The audit found no structural redundancy, no misplaced store, and no roadmap product that requires a redesign — only naming to reconcile, two concepts to re-class, and additive engines to build. The verdict is unambiguous: this is the correct foundation. Canonization closes not by adding to it, but by clearing it for expansion — with exactly three moves.

Move 01 · promote

Organization → subject

Give the network its second pole. An organization gets its own evidence, trust, and memory — the single addition that turns a record about clinicians into a network between clinicians and the places they work.

Move 02 · demote

Recognition · Credential → fold in

Stop minting primitives that already exist. Recognition is conferred evidence; a credential is evidence on a node. Folding both into the atom and the graph keeps the canon small and the store singular.

Move 03 · build

Transitivity engine

Light up the network effect. Write the one missing engine — trust that flows between careers through shared anchors — on top of the substrate, never under it. The last piece, and the only net-new foundation-level code.

Canon · frozen at W228The EvidenceObject is the atom and the only store. CareerEvent is its aggregate. Graph, Trust, Timeline, and Mobility are projections — computed, reversible, never stored. The subjects are Clinician, Organization, and Opportunity. The law is: store once, project many. Everything VitalCV builds from Wave 229 forward inherits this canon unchanged — because the audit's finding is that it does not need to change. The foundation is correct; the network is cleared to grow.
