STAGE 01 · OF 08

OAuth onboarding

Operator time ~60s

The clinician signs in through their institution's identity provider. VitalCV never holds a password. The session is bound to a verified email at the IdP. If the IdP revokes the user, the VitalCV session is dead at the next refresh — no separate offboarding.

What VitalCV does
  • Receives an OIDC id_token signed by the IdP, not by VitalCV.
  • Mints a short-lived vc_session bound to a DPoP key on the device.
  • Logs an auth.session.opened event into the audit chain.
What VitalCV does NOT do
  • Store passwords, OTP seeds, or biometric material.
  • Re-issue tokens when the IdP has revoked the upstream session.
  • Federate identity outside the operator's chosen IdP.
Boundary

Identity assurance lives at the IdP. VitalCV does not assert who the clinician is — it asserts what authoritative registries say about the NPI the clinician claims. If the IdP says "this is mmiller@cedarhealth.org," VitalCV records that, and binds the passport to it. Nothing more.

Honest limitation
Verified email ≠ verified clinician. The NPI claim in Stage 02 is what binds this person to a real provider record. OAuth alone proves only that the user controlled an inbox at the time of login.
STAGE 02 · OF 08

NPI validation

Operator time ~15s

The clinician supplies a 10-digit NPI. VitalCV resolves it against the federal NPPES enumeration in a single direct call and stamps the response with an immutable receipt. A receipt is generated whether the NPI resolves or not. Failure is loud, not silent.

What this binds
  • The OAuth subject from Stage 01 to a federally enumerated provider.
  • The legal name and taxonomy in the passport to NPPES, not to clinician self-report.
  • A signed receipt that any auditor can replay against the same authority.
What this does NOT prove
  • That the clinician is licensed in any state. See Stage 03.
  • That the clinician is free of OIG exclusions. See Stage 03.
  • That the NPI was claimed by its lawful holder. Handled at IdP + identity-proofing layer.
Honest limitation
NPPES is self-attested by providers and updated on a rolling basis. A current NPPES record proves enumeration; it does not prove competence, license, or current employment. Treat it as the floor of identity, not the ceiling.

Why this is replayable
  • Every receipt commits the previous receipt's hash. Tamper with row N → rows N…end invalidate.
  • The chain head is signed quarterly with an RFC3161 timestamp from an external TSA.
  • Verification requires only the issuer's did:web public key — no live API call.
What the chain is NOT
  • A blockchain. There is no token, no consensus, no global ledger.
  • A substitute for source-of-truth. It proves what was observed, not what is true now.
  • Confidential. The chain commits hashes, not PHI. PHI lives encrypted under operator-owned keys.
STAGE 04 · OF 08

What the signed credential carries
  • The lane states at issuance — Verified / Pending / Gated.
  • The hash root of the receipt chain that produced those states.
  • A Status List pointer the verifier checks for revocation.
What it does NOT carry
  • Bulk PHI, full transcripts, or any document attachments. Pointers only.
  • A score. The credential is a provenance map, not a rank.
  • An indefinite assertion. After expiry, treat as advisory.
Honest limitation
The badge says: "as of 2026-05-11 09:43 UTC, these registries returned these states." It does not say "this clinician is safe to credential today." That conclusion remains the operator's, performed against your own policy — see Stage 08.
STAGE 05 · OF 08

Verifier validation

Operator time ~90s

A third party — locum agency, partner hospital, MSO — pastes the badge into the public verifier. All checks run locally in the verifier's browser against the issuer's published public key. The verifier never calls VitalCV's API for the verdict; it calls the underlying registries directly.

Boundary

The verifier holds the trust, not VitalCV. If VitalCV's servers were offline tomorrow, this verification still completes — the verifier has the public key, the token, the Status List, and the source registries. The trust is in the cryptography, not in our availability.

Honest limitation
A "Pass" verdict means: the badge is cryptographically genuine and has not been revoked or expired. It does not mean the clinician's credentials are valid today. For PECOS and gated state boards, the verifier sees VitalCV's last-observed state, not real-time. Stage 06 covers what happens when that state changes.
STAGE 06 · OF 08

Revocation continuity

Operator time ~60s

Revocation is fail-closed. The issuer flips a bit in the published Status List. Any future verification — even on a cached badge in a hiring manager's inbox — reads that bit and flips to "do not rely on this badge." No code change at the verifier. No notification dance.

Re-issuance policyA revoked badge is never un-revoked. The clinician requests a new badge after state cures; a fresh chain begins.
What this guarantees
  • Every prior screenshot of the badge is now visibly stale on re-verification.
  • Operators relying on cached state see REVOKED within the Status List TTL.
  • The revocation receipt is itself a chain row — auditable like any other event.
What this does NOT do
  • Notify every past verifier individually. The Status List pull model is the spec.
  • Erase the badge. Revocation is additive — the original record stands, marked stale.
  • Block re-application. The clinician can request a fresh badge once the underlying issue cures.
Honest limitation
Worst-case propagation lag is 15 minutes — the Status List TTL. A determined attacker presenting a cached, freshly-revoked badge in that window will fool a verifier that does not force-refresh. For high-stakes decisions (privileges, prescribing authority), operators should explicitly force a Status List re-fetch — Stage 08 documents the policy hook.
STAGE 07 · OF 08

Audit exports

Operator time ~90s

A compliance officer requests the proof dossier. VitalCV emits a single tarball with a published manifest. Every file inside is hashed, the manifest itself is signed, and verification requires only the issuer DID document. No live call to VitalCV is required to validate the export.

What the auditor can do offline
  • Verify manifest.json signature against the issuer DID document.
  • Replay the receipt chain row-by-row and confirm the root hash.
  • Re-check the RFC3161 anchor against the published TSA root.
What the bundle does NOT contain
  • Live API keys, session tokens, or operator credentials.
  • Documents the operator never uploaded (resumes, COIs, etc. require explicit opt-in).
  • Third-party PII outside the credentialing subject — references and peers are redacted by default.
Honest limitation
The bundle is a tar.gz, not a magic certificate. A regulator who does not run the verification commands has only our word that it's valid. The whole point of publishing the manifest format is that they don't have to take our word — but they do have to run the commands. README.txt is seven lines.
STAGE 08 · OF 08

Trust boundaries

Operator time ~90s

The single most important slide. VitalCV is in the trust path for receipt issuance. VitalCV is NOT in the trust path for verifier acceptance. Confusing the two is the failure mode every institutional reviewer should test us against.

Zone A · 01
Operator's IdP
Owns identity. Owns MFA. Owns offboarding. VitalCV reads ID tokens from this zone but does not issue them.
In trust path
Zone B · 02
VitalCV CVO
Fetches authoritative registries, signs receipts, mints the badge. Trusted to observe and record — not to decide.
In trust path
Zone C · 03
Verifier & auditor
Holds the issuer public key, fetches the Status List, replays the chain. Does not call VitalCV to render its verdict.
Out of trust path
Issuance side →
Trust boundary │
→ Verification side
What we ask you to trust us on
  • The integrity of the receipt at the moment of observation. We sign what we saw.
  • The honesty of our lane state model — Verified / Pending / Gated / Contradicted / Blocked.
  • The accuracy of our NCQA CR mapping. Wrong mapping is a bug, not a posture.
What we do NOT ask
  • That you trust our API uptime for verification. The cryptography survives our outage.
  • That you treat VitalCV's lane states as a credentialing decision. They are inputs to your decision.
  • That you skip your own committee review. Initial screening is not full primary-source verification.
Boundary

If you fire us tomorrow, the badges already issued stay verifiable. The public key, the chain, the Status List — all are published artifacts. We do not hold operators hostage to our continuity. That is the only durable form of institutional trust.

Honest limitation
Two scenarios where VitalCV is unavoidably in the trust path: (1) the period between a real-world status change and the next registry fetch, and (2) any lane VitalCV originates itself (e.g. clinician-uploaded documents tagged "Inferred"). For both, the lane carries explicit state and provenance tags. Treat anything not marked verified as advisory.

Ten minutes, in one line

VitalCV resolves a clinician's NPI against federal authority, records every check as a hash-linked receipt, mints a short-lived Verifiable Credential, and publishes a Status List for revocation. A third-party verifier can confirm the whole chain in their own browser without calling our API. An auditor can replay the whole chain offline from a published tarball. Where we do not have authority — current real-time license state at a gated board, identity at the IdP, your own credentialing decision — we say so, by name, on the surface where it applies.
