04

Remaining visual-semantic conflicts

five conflicts · two critical · three drift
Critical · Passport

Passport asserts authority without showing the signer.

The passport surface reads as a credential artifact — solid lane rows, freshness chips, mono IDs — but the signer, the replay anchor, and the chain head are not above the fold. A reviewer landing here would conclude “credential” without ever seeing the signing key. Same posture as a screenshot of a credential.
Remediation
Add a signature footer strip to the passport, identical render to Verifier Reading Mode’s issuer-continuity panel — collapsed by default, but always visible. Surface chain head + key fingerprint within the first 600 px.
Critical · Passport · degraded

Stale lanes render as muted instead of dashed.

The passport renders unfetched-recently lanes with reduced opacity. Without the dashed border (canon for degraded), a stale-but-valid value reads as “ok, just secondary” — when it should read as “not yet checked.” This is the exact conflation Failure Taxonomy mode D ≠ A is built to prevent.
Remediation
Replace opacity with dashed chip + explicit age. Stale never recolors — it borders.
Drift · Export Packet

Manifest header reorders generated_by before checked_at.

Reader hits who generated this before when was the source consulted. The canon says object → ownership → checked_at; generated_by belongs to the channel primitive (line 04), not above the timestamp.
Remediation
Reorder export-packet manifest to match canon. Move generated_by to position 4. checked_at is line 3, always.
Drift · Passport · masthead

NPI rendered in Geist instead of Geist Mono.

The masthead has “Dr. Mira Chen, MD · NPI 1356428912” with the NPI in sans. Per primitive 06, cryptographic and operational IDs (NPI, DID, hash, run_id) all share the mono identity treatment. Sans NPI reads as decorative metadata, not a stable identifier.
Remediation
All NPIs render mono with tabular-nums. Apply id-sample chip treatment.
Drift · Replay Memory · timeline

Timeline timestamps split into two micro-types — drifts from canon.

The timeline renders absolute UTC and relative age in two different font weights but at the same size and case. The canon mandates a stricter stack: absolute mono / relative micro-caps below. Reads close; not identical to Verifier Reading Mode and Signed Receipt.
Remediation
Adopt the .when + .sub stack from Verifier Reading Mode. Single source of truth for timestamp rendering.

06

Institutional legitimacy · risk areas

where an auditor would hesitate
Risk · A

Passport reads as the credential itself.

An auditor screenshotting the passport could circulate a frame with no signer, no chain head, and no replay anchor. The image carries authority by visual register alone. The system can’t prevent screenshots — but it can ensure no above-the-fold view of any surface implies a credential without rendering the signer.
Risk · B

Stale ≈ verified, when degradation is muted.

Mode A (source unreachable) and Mode D (no adverse findings) are the easiest pair to conflate. Any surface that renders stale as muted-but-positive-looking is a reportable epistemic failure for an institutional reviewer. The canon’s dashed-border rule must be enforced everywhere, not stylistically.
Risk · C

Reading order drift = audit-defensibility drift.

If generated_by appears before checked_at on any surface, a reviewer can argue the artifact privileges authorship over evidence. NCQA CR §3 and downstream MSO compliance teams read these surfaces left-to-right; order is the assertion.
Risk · D

One inversion · used inconsistently.

The inverted dark register is reserved for the cryptographic plane — Signed Receipt, Signature Panel, Lineage Strip. If it leaks into hero panels, KPI strips, or marketing surfaces, the reserved meaning dilutes. The visual grammar loses its single most institutional signal.
Risk · E

SaaS dashboard tropes in KPI strips.

Big-number cards · gradient backgrounds · progress rings · positive-delta arrows in green. These read as product analytics, not evidence. Operational metrics belong in a mono table with units and source-fetch timestamps, not in dashboard cards.
Risk · F

"VC 2.0 compatible · OpenID4VCI aligned" as a trust strip.

Standards-conformance phrasing that’s tactically marketing is the single line most likely to discount the whole surface in front of an institutional reader. Replace with a literal pointer to the conformance assertion (see /.well-known/openid-credential-issuer) or remove.
07

Recommendations · before verifier-grade rollout

six commitments · in order of dependency
Canon v1 · binding on all verifier-facing artifacts
  1. 01
    Enforce reading order at the component layer.Build the eight consolidated components (§05) and forbid surface authors from arranging the primitives in any other order. The canon is a render, not a guideline. Single source of truth.
  2. 02
    Make ownership a mandatory rendered token, not an inference.Every claim row carries an OwnershipBadge. The implicit case (“the user is logged in, so it’s their data”) is the most dangerous form of trust assumption. Render it.
  3. 03
    Add a SignaturePanel to the Passport · collapsed default.The single largest legitimacy gap. The Passport currently asserts authority without ever rendering the signer above the fold. Add the panel, collapsed, but ensure key fingerprint + chain head are visible in the first 600 px.
  4. 04
    Replace all opacity-based degradation with dashed semantics.Audit every surface for opacity: 0.6 applied to stale data. Replace with the canonical dashed chip + explicit age + stale-flag. Opacity carries no epistemic content; borders do.
  5. 05
    Reserve the inverted register strictly for the cryptographic plane.Audit every dark surface. If it’s not signed-receipt, signature-panel, or lineage-strip, lighten it. The inversion is the system’s single most legible institutional signal — don’t spend it on chrome.
  6. 06
    Strip standards-as-marketing language.Remove every “VC 2.0 compatible · audit-ready · OpenID4VCI aligned” trust strip. Replace with literal pointers to conformance assertions. The auditor reads the URL, not the adjective.
