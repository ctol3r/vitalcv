03 ·

Hierarchy + typography rationale

why this reads as evidence, not a card
Hierarchy
  • Verdict before content. A verifier should not have to read the claim to know whether to trust the receipt. The verdict pill (Verifiable) and the state stamp (SIGNED) are the first and last reading anchors of the head row.
  • Receipt ID is co-equal with subject. The institutional identity of the artifact is the hash, not the person. Both render in 15 px monospace; the subject's human name is the sub-label, not the title.
  • Lineage before claim. Provenance precedes assertion. The 6-cell lineage rail is between verdict and content — a reviewer cannot reach the claim without crossing it.
  • Cryptography before prose. DID / kid / hash / status occupy a full-width 4-column block above the attested claim. Cryptographic continuity is a precondition for reading, not a footnote.
  • Sources separated from issuer. Each source is named in its own KV cell under the claim. The page never permits a reader to conflate "issuer" with "authority."
  • Custody in inverted register. The chain of custody renders dark — visually reserved for the cryptographic plane. A reviewer learns once that "dark = signed" and applies the rule everywhere.
  • Verify URL on the artifact. The receipt names its own re-derivation path. Trust is portable to a reviewer's own tooling.
Typography
  • Every machine identifier is monospace, tabular-nums. Hashes, DIDs, key IDs, timestamps, NPIs, license numbers. The reader never has to disambiguate 0 from O.
  • Geist Mono in four sizes only. 9.5 px (keys), 11.5 px (values), 13 px (verdict facts), 18 px (verdict / claim). No fifth identifier size.
  • Geist Sans for prose only. The attested-claim sentence is sans because it is meant to be read, not pattern-matched. Everything around it is monospace because it is meant to be verified.
  • Eyebrow tracking 0.18 em uppercase. Marks every "this is a label, not a value." Reviewer learns the eyebrow on first encounter; the rule applies on every surface.
  • No accent colour. Single ink axis (paper → ink-50 → ink-900 → ink-950). Emphasis comes from weight (500 → 600), background (paper → ink), or border thickness (1 px → 2 px). Never from hue.
  • No animation. No shadow. No gradient. The artifact is permanent. The page never apologises for being still.
04 ·

Verifier readability — pre-explanation trust

"trust before reading"
Success condition · "A verifier trusts the receipt before reading the explanation text."
  • First fixation · the verdict pill Verifiable on ink-950 with the σ-ok sub-line. Reads in < 1 s. No prose required.
  • Second fixation · the receipt hash on the same row. A reviewer who already trusts the issuer DID can verify the hash against their own ledger before reading anything else.
  • Third fixation · the state stamp SIGNED (inverted). Three independent confirmations of the same fact: verdict, σ-ok, stamp. Trust is established in triplicate before the claim sentence renders.
  • Fourth fixation · the cryptographic block. Key fingerprint, DID document status, status-list status — all 200 OK, all monospace, all in one row. A reviewer sees that the signing apparatus is operationally continuous.
  • The attested claim itself is the fifth thing on the page. By the time the reviewer reaches it, they have already decided whether to trust the artifact. The prose is confirmation, not foundation.

Verdict: success condition met. The receipt establishes its own trustworthiness through machine-verifiable anchors (verdict, hash, σ, stamp, cryptography) before any human-readable narrative is presented. Reviewers who choose to skip the prose lose nothing operationally — the artifact is still verifiable. Reviewers who read the prose find it consistent with what the anchors already asserted.
