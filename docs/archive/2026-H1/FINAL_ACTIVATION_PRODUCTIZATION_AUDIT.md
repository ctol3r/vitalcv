# Final Activation Productization Audit

Scope: homepage, passport, onboarding, and deployment clarity after the activation refinement pass.

This audit is product-facing only. It does not introduce new systems or expand runtime topology.

## 1. What still feels procedural

- The deepest passport sections still read like evidence blocks, even when the top summary is calm.
- Onboarding still contains supporting copy for preview and activation continuation, which is useful but slightly more detailed than the home screen.
- Some deployment truth still depends on reading a structured object rather than a single plain-language sentence.

## 2. What still weakens onboarding momentum

- Guest preview and readiness loading still create a brief pause before the final step appears.
- The homepage promise is better, but it still asks the user to understand one more concept: the passport snapshot.
- Some supporting descriptions still explain the transition instead of simply carrying it forward.

## 3. What still feels like enterprise tooling

- The passport lower sections remain rich and operational, which can feel more like an internal review surface than a pure consumer activation experience.
- Source coverage and evidence panels are readable, but they are still denser than the top summary.
- Runtime health is clearer now, but it still behaves like an operator console by necessity.

## 4. What still weakens emotional trust

- Any remaining setup, provisioning, or process-oriented wording dilutes the calm identity-first story.
- The product is strongest when it says what is ready, what is waiting, and what the next step is.
- Dense supporting evidence can make the experience feel heavier than the promise at the top of the funnel.

## 5. What still visually fragments the experience

- The passport mixes high-confidence summary cards with denser proof sections.
- Onboarding is calmer, but it still uses a distinct visual rhythm from the homepage.
- Loading states are smoother, though not fully invisible.

## 6. What still creates cognitive overload

- Multiple evidence sections below the fold can compete for attention after the top summary has already answered the main question.
- A few adjacent surfaces still use legacy vocabulary that is useful to operators but not necessary for a new clinician.
- The runtime health payload remains operator-readable rather than consumer-simple, which is correct but still dense.

## 7. Highest-leverage remaining simplifications

- Keep the top passport summary as the dominant decision surface and treat everything below it as supporting evidence.
- Continue removing the last pockets of setup/provisioning language from adjacent activation surfaces.
- Tighten homepage copy to keep the promise immediate and the explanation minimal.
- Keep deployment truth explicit, but limit it to canonical, preview, stale, or unavailable.

## 8. Top remaining conversion opportunities

- Reduce the amount of explanatory text between homepage entry and the passport result.
- Make onboarding feel even more like a continuation of the passport rather than a separate step.
- Push calm decision labels higher in the passport layout.
- Keep the next-step framing consistent across homepage, passport, and onboarding so the experience feels continuous.

## Bottom line

VitalCV now reads like a credible institutional activation product.

The remaining work is not architectural. It is editorial and hierarchical:

- fewer words
- clearer decision labels
- less evidence noise above the fold
- more direct next-step framing

That is the remaining productization frontier.
