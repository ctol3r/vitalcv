# Productization Gap Audit V4

Scope: homepage, passport, onboarding, and deployment truth after the continuity refinement pass.

This audit is intentionally product-facing. It does not expand runtime systems or payload complexity.

## 1. What still feels procedural

- The deepest passport sections still carry evidence-driven structure, which is necessary but still more procedural than the top summary.
- Onboarding still has a few explanatory transitions, especially around the short loading bridge before activation continues.
- Runtime health remains operator-readable and therefore more structured than consumer-simple.

## 2. What still weakens activation momentum

- Any pause between homepage entry and the first passport result is still noticeable, even when the copy is calm.
- The onboarding bridge is smoother now, but it still uses a visible progress step rather than a fully invisible continuation.
- The user still needs to absorb that the passport is the next operational step, which is clear but not yet perfectly frictionless.

## 3. What still feels like enterprise tooling

- The lower passport sections remain detailed enough to read like a review surface if scanned all at once.
- Source coverage and evidence panels are still denser than the top summary, which is correct operationally but still tooling-like.
- Deployment truth is now readable, but it still surfaces as a structured operator summary rather than a single sentence.

## 4. What still weakens emotional trust

- Any remaining setup, provisioning, or process language is still too close to the old workflow mental model.
- The product is strongest when it says what is ready, what is waiting, and what the next step is.
- Dense evidence sections can dilute the emotional punch of the top-level promise if a user keeps scrolling.

## 5. What still visually fragments the experience

- The passport combines a calm summary area with denser supporting proof, which creates a visible hierarchy shift.
- Onboarding and homepage now align better, but they still have different visual rhythms.
- Loading states are quieter than before, yet they still create a small break in the flow.

## 6. What still creates cognitive overload

- Multiple support sections below the fold can compete with the top-level trust answer.
- A few adjacent surfaces still use legacy vocabulary that is helpful for operators but unnecessary for a first-time clinician.
- The deployment summary remains legible, but it still requires reading more than one field to classify the runtime.

## 7. Highest-leverage remaining simplifications

- Keep the passport top summary as the primary decision surface and treat everything below it as support.
- Continue removing any remaining setup/provisioning language from adjacent surfaces.
- Keep the homepage promise short and forward-moving.
- Keep deployment truth to the smallest set of human-readable classes that still stay honest.

## 8. Strongest remaining conversion opportunities

- Shorten the time between homepage action and first passport signal.
- Make onboarding feel even more like a continuation of the passport snapshot.
- Keep the decision label visible at the top of passport so employers can move in seconds.
- Preserve the same next-step language across homepage, passport, and onboarding so the funnel feels continuous.

## Bottom line

VitalCV now reads like a credible institutional activation product.

The remaining gains are mostly editorial:

- fewer words
- fewer transitional reminders
- clearer top-level decision labels
- less evidence noise above the fold

That is the remaining productization work.
