# Final Productization Readiness Audit

Scope: homepage, passport, onboarding, and deployment truth after the continuity refinement pass.

This is a product-readiness audit, not a systems audit.

## 1. What still feels procedural

- The passport still contains detailed support sections below the summary card, which are operationally correct but more procedural than the top snapshot.
- Onboarding still has a short loading bridge and explanatory copy to keep continuity explicit.
- Deployment truth is concise now, but it still reads as an operator classification instead of a single consumer-facing sentence.

## 2. What still weakens activation momentum

- Any pause between entering the homepage and seeing the passport result is still perceptible.
- The onboarding bridge is calm, but it still uses visible progress rather than disappearing entirely.
- Some support text still explains the transition instead of simply carrying the user forward.

## 3. What still feels like enterprise tooling

- The lower passport sections are still dense enough to read like an internal review surface if a user scrolls past the top summary.
- Source coverage and evidence details are useful, but they are still heavier than the above-the-fold decision layer.
- Runtime health remains intentionally operator-readable, so it still feels like a control surface rather than a simple product badge.

## 4. What still weakens emotional trust

- Any remaining setup, provisioning, or workflow-adjacent language pulls the experience back toward old tooling energy.
- The product is strongest when it says what is ready, what is waiting, and what happens next.
- Dense evidence blocks below the fold can dilute the emotional clarity established by the homepage and the passport summary.

## 5. What still visually fragments the experience

- The passport mixes a calm top summary with denser evidence below it, which creates a visible rhythm change.
- Homepage and onboarding now align better, but they still have slightly different pacing and visual density.
- Loading states are quieter than before, though they are still visibly present.

## 6. What still creates cognitive overload

- Multiple support sections below the fold can compete with the primary decision answer.
- A few adjacent surfaces still use legacy vocabulary that is accurate for operators but not necessary for a first-time clinician.
- Deployment truth is readable, but it still asks the operator to classify a few fields rather than glance at one word.

## 7. Strongest remaining conversion opportunities

- Reduce the time between homepage entry and passport answer.
- Keep the top passport summary as the only thing a recruiter must read to decide next action.
- Make onboarding feel even more like a direct continuation of the passport snapshot.
- Keep the same next-step language across homepage, passport, and onboarding so the funnel feels continuous.

## 8. Highest-leverage remaining simplifications

- Trim the remaining support copy that explains the transition instead of carrying it.
- Keep the decision label visible and dominant above the fold.
- Preserve the calm, source-backed promise language and remove any leftover old workflow tone.
- Keep deployment truth to four simple classes: canonical production, canonical preview, stale legacy, unavailable.

## Bottom line

VitalCV now reads like a believable institutional identity platform.

The remaining gains are editorial and hierarchical:

- fewer words
- shorter transition bridges
- clearer top-level decision labels
- less evidence noise above the fold

That is the remaining productization work.
