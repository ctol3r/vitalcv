# Final Experience Coherence Audit

VitalCV now feels like one continuous identity activation flow: homepage to passport to onboarding.

The remaining work is not structural. It is mostly about keeping the experience calm, short, and easy to follow.

## 1. What still feels procedural

- The passport still exposes multiple supporting sections below the main trust read.
- Onboarding still includes a small amount of step narration, even when the handoff is smooth.
- Runtime health remains an operator surface, which is useful but inherently procedural.

## 2. What still weakens onboarding momentum

- Any extra explanatory sentence slows the handoff a little.
- Fallback states still need a moment to explain what is happening.
- Transition screens can still feel like a checkpoint instead of a continuation.

## 3. What still feels like enterprise tooling

- Source coverage panels and blocker lists are still decision-support surfaces.
- Deployment truth is still operational metadata, even when it is concise.
- The passport still contains detail blocks that read like supporting evidence, not just the verdict.

## 4. What still weakens emotional trust

- Over-qualifying a state makes the product feel careful instead of assured.
- Too many support labels can soften the main trust signal.
- Any visible waiting language can make the experience feel less inevitable.

## 5. What still visually fragments the experience

- The homepage, passport, and onboarding screens each have their own supporting card patterns.
- Secondary rows and badges can still compete with the main decision line.
- Fallback and loading states still use a different visual rhythm.

## 6. What still creates cognitive overload

- The passport can still present more information than a first-pass reader needs.
- Supporting state groups are useful, but they add scan work.
- Transition content can still ask the user to read more than necessary before moving on.

## 7. Strongest remaining conversion opportunities

- Keep compressing the top passport section until the first read is enough.
- Keep reducing step narration on loading and transition surfaces.
- Use the same short vocabulary everywhere for identity, readiness, waiting, and next step.
- Preserve one primary action per surface.

## 8. Highest-leverage remaining simplifications

- Remove duplicate explanatory sentences wherever the UI already shows the state.
- Keep the passport trust header decisive and brief.
- Keep deployment truth short enough to scan in one glance.
- Keep onboarding as continuation, not setup.
- Keep homepage copy short enough to feel inevitable.

## Coherence View

- The product is coherent.
- The remaining work is to keep it short enough to stay calm.
- The main risk now is editorial drift, not architectural drift.
