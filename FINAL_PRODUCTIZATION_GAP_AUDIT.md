# Final Productization Gap Audit

Scope: homepage, passport, onboarding, and deployment clarity after the activation/productization pass.

This audit is limited to product feel and operational readability. It does not expand infrastructure or trust topology.

## 1. What still feels prototype-like

- A few adjacent surfaces still use older vocabulary such as `workflow`, `setup`, or `decision-grade` in places that are not part of the core activation funnel.
- The passport experience is calmer now, but the full page still contains a lot of supporting evidence sections that read like a technical dossier when scanned end to end.
- Some loading states still lean on generic skeletons and motion, which is acceptable, but they are not yet fully invisible.

## 2. What still weakens onboarding momentum

- Onboarding still contains multiple supporting explanations around guest mode and preview flow.
- The final activation path is calm, but it still has more steps and state text than a truly minimal identity-first funnel.
- The handoff between passport and onboarding is clear, but there is still room to reduce the amount of supporting copy between the CTA and the first active step.

## 3. What still feels like enterprise tooling

- The passport’s lower sections remain highly detailed and can read like an internal review console if a reviewer scrolls past the top summary.
- Source coverage and evidence panels are operationally correct, but some of the wording is still closer to an analyst tool than a consumer-grade trust surface.
- The runtime health surface is operator-readable, but it still exposes more internal state than a pure product dashboard would.

## 4. What still weakens emotional trust

- Any remaining technical language that references setup, provisioning, or process overhead adds friction.
- The product is strongest when it says what is ready, what is waiting, and what the next step is. Anything beyond that dilutes confidence.
- Dense evidence sections can make the experience feel heavier than the homepage promise, even when the underlying state is honest.

## 5. What still visually fragments the experience

- The passport page mixes calm product summary cards with denser proof sections, which creates a shift in visual rhythm.
- Some supporting surfaces still use different density rules and card patterns, so the overall experience is not yet perfectly uniform.
- Loading and preview states are better than before, but they still create a subtle transition break rather than feeling fully continuous.

## 6. Top remaining conversion opportunities

- Compress the passport top summary even further so the first screen answers the entire trust question faster.
- Reduce the amount of supporting explanation between homepage CTA and the first meaningful passport result.
- Make the onboarding handoff feel even more like a continuation of the passport snapshot, not a new flow.

## 7. Top remaining enterprise trust weaknesses

- A few legacy surfaces still carry older product language that can confuse the new institutional story.
- The deeper evidence sections are accurate, but they still require more effort to scan than the top-level trust summary.
- Operator-facing deployment truth is improving, but it still depends on reading a structured payload rather than a single canonical sentence.

## 8. Highest-leverage remaining simplifications

- Remove or rewrite the last pockets of setup/provisioning language across adjacent surfaces.
- Keep the passport top summary as the primary decision surface and push supporting evidence one level lower in the hierarchy.
- Reduce supporting copy in onboarding to the minimum required for confidence.
- Keep deployment truth explicit, but not verbose: canonical, preview, stale, or unavailable.

## Bottom line

VitalCV now reads much more like a calm professional identity platform than an infrastructure portal.

The biggest remaining gains are not architectural. They are:

- fewer words
- clearer hierarchy
- fewer technical reminders
- more immediate trust posture visibility

That is the remaining productization work.
