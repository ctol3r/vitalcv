What the verdict commits the whole product to

Full memo: DECISION-MEMO.md. The winner is not a homepage — it is the workspace, the employer surface, and the evidence artifact for the next two years.

A — Operational Calm

  • Light bone product everywhere; cheapest migration from today's paper+ink. Instrument Sans + IBM Plex Mono.
  • Spruce becomes both the brand accent and the work/done color — one green, one meaning; the separate indigo interaction accent retires.
  • Trade-off: distinctiveness is "partial by design" — the calm thesis bets the work is the memorable thing.

B — Intelligent Product

  • Dark-first public brand (warm graphite) — overturns "light is the only public mode" and requires defining where light bands are law. Geist + Geist Mono.
  • The hero's living work surface is the grammar the product itself adopts (work ledger, approval pauses) — the demonstration is the brand.
  • Creates one real design task: a defined light/print treatment for evidence artifacts an employer forwards.

C — Precision / Editorial

  • Gallery white + true black drafted rules product-wide; expanded Archivo display discipline at product density is a genuinely new design problem.
  • Cleanest signal-vs-state separation: vermilion is architectural only, states stay black/white.
  • Trade-off: the far pole from calm — daily-use fatigue in the workspace is the open question.

Named hybrids

  • "A ground, B hero" — bone/spruce system with B's living work surface (needs a re-render on bone to prove it).
  • "A system, C eyebrow" — operational calm with C's drafted two-rule band for character.
  • "C public, A workspace" — two registers; only lawful if written into the constitution deliberately.

Standing recommendation

B — Intelligent Product: the program's thesis is "show, don't assert," and B is the only direction where the first experience is the product doing work. Its two costs are bounded design tasks, not truth risks. If maximum calm wins instead: A, or "A ground, B hero."
